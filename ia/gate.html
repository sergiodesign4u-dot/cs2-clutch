The one thing this node is not

Identity verification. The split is D-17, and it is measured rather than argued.

Two layers, and the second one is somewhere else

Layer 1 was an 18+ self-declaration here. Layer 2 was identity verification before funding, at 2.7. The declaration is cheap on purpose and meets every visitor at the door, where conversion is most fragile. The expensive check meets the person at the money moment, where they are already committed and where the law actually attaches.

Both halves of that sentence have moved, and it is kept as the argument rather than as the rule. D-26 took the declaration off this node and put it in the consent gate of 2.4. The same decision took layer 2 out of round 1 and moved it from before funding to before withdrawal. So this node is not the cheap half of a two-layer split any more: it is the market question, and round 1 has exactly one age control and no identity control at all.

And the split is counted rather than asserted. The map puts the main job at three taps and this gate is one of them. The compliance layer eats a third of the budget for the main job. If identity verification sat at this tap the main job would be unreachable inside budget for a first-time visitor, which is why D-19 records the split and the tap count as one decision.

The honest limit, stated here rather than left for an audit to find

A self-declaration is not verification. aarrr.md already discarded soft age gating standing alone, and research.md section 7 records that checkbox age confirmation stopped being lawful for gambling sites in the UK from 25 July 2025. The UK is blocked at launch, so nothing is broken today.

What breaks is the day a market opens whose age method forbids a declaration, and that is exactly why age method is a field in 0.12 rather than a constant here. The word "only" in "the 18+ declaration only" is about scope, not about the number: Belgium is 21.

When it fires, and why never on arrival

Row B3 says "before any case interaction", not before any interaction, and the difference is the whole pre-login argument.

The category does the opposite, and it was opened live

The block bank records Hellcase serving a sign-in modal on arrival, before any content, carrying Steam, Google and Telegram, a terms agreement, and "I'm at the age of legal capacity (usually, 18 years) or older" as a checkbox inside it.

That is two barriers in one component: the trust evaluation cannot happen at all, and the age declaration is demoted to a line in a signup form. Verdict LEAVE, against B3 and D-17.

Market first, then age. The second half left with D-26, and the argument is kept

The 18+ declaration moved to the sign-in consent gate on 2.4. The ordering argument below is kept because it is the first thing anyone re-derives if the decision is revisited.

Two reasons, both structural.

1. The minimum age is a property of the market row. 0.12 makes minimum age and age method fields precisely because they are not universal. We cannot ask the correct question before we know which row applies.

2. Asking a person to declare their age and then refusing them the market spends their statement for nothing, and collects a declaration we have no basis to hold. A refusal that arrives after a person has answered reads as a bait, which is the shape of B8-4 at a smaller scale.

The four rules, and they are the register's rather than this node's

  1. A reason, always. "Not available in your country" with no ground reads as arbitrary because it is indistinguishable from arbitrary.
  2. Readable words, not a citation dump. The instrument lives in the register. The person gets one sentence they can act on.
  3. What is still open, stated in the same breath. Reading the site, the legal pages, support, and, where an account exists, the withdrawal.
  4. Never a soft 404 and never an error. 0.3 holds the error surfaces and this is not one of them.

200 with content, and it is an HTTP fact rather than a preference

Never 404, never 403, never a redirect to an error page. The reason is doubled: it is an answer rather than a missing page, and because the block renders at the URL the person is already on, there is nothing for a crawler to index and no market from which a crawler can be served the blocked page instead of the real one.

0.3 records the inverse failure live: Key-Drop returns HTTP 404 with the home page rendered in the body, so the person never learns the URL was wrong and the crawler drops the page. Both readers misled by one response. A geo block dressed as a 403 is the same defect pointed at a different audience.

A dead end for the right person is a success. For the wrong person it is a defect

Wrongly blocked gets the route to 0.10, which carries row G4: a stated ground and an appeal with a published response deadline. The deadline is [?] and it is 0.10's, not invented here.

Wrongly allowed is the expensive one and it is not this node's: later evidence at 4.1 or 2.7 moves the account to closing, where withdrawal stays open and the balance is frozen rather than zeroed.

What this state must never say

"Blocked" when the row is not launched, because that is a legal verdict we do not hold. A statute number as the whole message, because it is the ground's source and not the ground. "Temporarily unavailable" when nothing about it is temporary. An error code, a stack trace, or the word error. A list of the markets that are open: the footer's market statement is the public face of the register and it is crawlable everywhere, and this dialog answers one person's question rather than publishing the register.

State 2.3. Under age. Dissolved by D-26, and kept for the argument

Why it is gone, and why the section stays

The age control is now two checkboxes on 2.4 that a person either ticks or does not. A declaration you decline to make is not a refusal that can be recorded, so there is no under-age event, nothing to store and no screen to draw. What replaces it is 2.4's own state, consent not given.

The section stays because the distinction it drew, between declining to answer and declaring under 18, is the reason this node existed at all, and it is the first thing anyone will re-derive if the decision is revisited. A deleted argument cannot be reread.

What stays open, and why it is not everything

Reading a public page is not a case interaction, so 1.2, 0.9, 6.1 and 0.10 remain reachable. Every case control does not. 6.1 and 0.10 are named in the copy because the one thing a person in this state might actually need is a route to a human.

How long the decline persists and what it is keyed to are [?], because both are production and privacy questions this repository has not researched. What is fixed is that it is not one dialog dismissal.

A contrast that is load bearing for stage 04

2.5 Steam refused allows a retry and this state does not. A failed sign in is a fact about an identity provider. An under-age declaration is a statement the person made about themselves, and re-asking it is not a retry, it is coaching. The two states look similar and behave in opposite directions, so they do not share one component.

Blocks 1 to 3 fit above the fold at 360px. A declaration a person has to scroll to find has been designed as an obstacle rather than a question. If something must fall below, it is block 5, never block 3.

The two controls stack and never differ in visual weight. That is 0.4's reject-as-easy-as-accept rule applied to the second interrupt, and stacking is what stops the decline control from landing under a thumb that is already moving.

Desktop is the same order in a centred dialog, focus trapped, Escape closing it, the page behind it dimmed rather than removed: what the person was reading is the context for why they are being asked.

2.2 is not a dialog at 360px. They replace the dialog's content in place, because there is nothing to return to behind them.

No A to E block, and the reason is structural

This node has no URL, so it has no row in the indexation register, no canonical, no schema and nothing to index. What it owes SEO is three properties and all three are fixed elsewhere: it does not fire on arrival, so no public surface is gated for a person or a crawler; 2.2 returns 200 at the URL the person is already on, so the block cannot be indexed; one language, so no hreflang anywhere.

Accessibility, and two of these are compliance rather than craft. The ground is text, never an image and never colour alone, so a screen reader reads the reason and not only the refusal. The declaration is a real control with an accessible name that states the age. Focus moves into the dialog on open, is trapped, and returns to the trigger on dismissal. Escape closes it and records nothing. Every route out of 2.2 and 2.3 is a crawlable <a href>, because a destination reachable only by script does not exist for a keyboard either.
