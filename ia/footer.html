This node grew the map before it was specified

The pack's rule: a carrier may not promise a destination the map does not hold. This one promised six.

Two new nodes, registered before this specification was written

0.9 Legal and policy pages. Terms of use, Privacy policy, Cookie policy, Refund and payments policy. Four documents, one template: last updated date, a plain-language summary above the document, the document, the version history. They are one node because they are one template. Four specifications of the same page shape would be three copies. Each keeps its own URL and its own indexation.

0.10 Support and contact. The contact route Article 5(c) requires, plus the appeal route row G4 requires with its published response deadline. Not a policy page: it has a function and a service-level commitment, and G4 makes the deadline a testable acceptance criterion rather than a promise.

The legal ground, opened live rather than recalled

A legal requirement is read from the text of the law. Both quotes from EUR-Lex, opened 11 August 2026.

Directive 2000/31/EC, Article 5(1), general information to be provided

https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32000L0031
"Member States shall ensure that the service provider shall render easily, directly and permanently accessible to the recipients of the service and competent authorities, at least the following information: (a) the name of the service provider; (b) the geographic address at which the service provider is established; (c) the details of the service provider, including his electronic mail address, which allow him to be contacted rapidly and communicated with in a direct and effective manner; (d) where the service provider is registered in a trade or similar public register, the trade register in which the service provider is entered and his registration number; (e) where the activity is subject to an authorisation scheme, the particulars of the relevant supervisory authority; (g) where the service provider undertakes an activity that is subject to VAT, the identification number."

"Easily, directly and permanently accessible" is the footer's legal job description. Permanently accessible is what makes it a global element rather than an About page.

Article 1(5)(d), the exclusion

"This Directive shall not apply to: ... gambling activities which involve wagering a stake with monetary value in games of chance, including lotteries and betting transactions."

The open question does not have to be answered for this node to be built

CLAUDE.md records that whether this category counts as gambling is unsettled and varies by jurisdiction. That question is open, has an owner, and is not this node's to answer.

If the product is not gambling, Article 5 applies in full and the footer carries the identification block. If the product is gambling, this Directive does not apply, but then an authorisation regime does, and Article 5(e) already names the shape that regime demands: the particulars of the supervisory authority.

Both readings land on the same block in the same place. The structure is decided; only its contents wait, and what waits is written down: operating entity, registered address, register and number, VAT number, supervisory authority if there is one. All [?], all owned by the founder with counsel, riding on the same answer as D-A.

One candidate for the trust strip was considered and declined

The rolling median and p90 withdrawal time from our own logs, rows A4 and G3, is the strongest trust signal the product owns and it is visible before login. Both rows say in their own cells "same feature as A4, two surfaces", and those two are the entry surface and the withdrawal surface. Putting it on every page makes a third.

That is a scope change, not a layout choice, so it is named here with an owner rather than added quietly or dropped silently. Owner: the founder, and it costs one line in the backlog if the answer is yes.

The strip is not a badge row

No trust seals, no "100% secure", no invented certifications. Design principle 1 says trust is the product, and D-14 names the limit on what a trust claim can do: what answers the doubt people actually record is published chance, current value and tested RTP. A seal answers none of it.

SEO and accessibility

The second interlinking plane, and the one that carries the long tail.

Two planes, two hops

The rail carries three or four destinations. The footer carries every indexed node the rail cannot hold, plus the legal set. Together they mean every indexed page is reachable from every other page in at most two hops.

No H1. A global element is not a page. <footer> with <nav> around each link column, each with an accessible name. Four unnamed navigation landmarks in one region is worse than none.

The accordions are collapsed for reading, never for existence

Every link is in the DOM at every width and the accordion toggles visibility. This is the same rule 0.1 applies to the mobile drawer, and it is stated twice on purpose: it is the single most common way both patterns are shipped wrong.

Accessibility

  • Each accordion header is a <button> with aria-expanded and aria-controls, not a styled <div>
  • Every target at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, matching 0.1's AAA choice
  • The identification block is text, not an image. A registered address in a graphic satisfies nobody, including Article 5's "easily, directly and permanently accessible"
  • Link text is descriptive on its own: "Refund and payments policy", never "read more" or "here"
  • The trust strip is not aria-hidden and not decorative. It carries the age statement, which is a compliance statement and not ornament

Indexation of what it points at. 0.9 and 0.10 are indexed and carry schema. The private nodes are absent entirely, so no nofollow is needed anywhere: the correct way not to pass a link is not to draw it.
