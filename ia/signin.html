The carrier is a dialog, and the address is its cold arrival

D-54, 21 August 2026, founder decision, taken before the screen was drawn. Sign in stops being a page a person is sent to. It is a dialog opened over the surface they are already on, drawn once as a canon and mounted wherever a guest meets a control they cannot use.

The argument, and it is smaller than it looks

A dialog does not remove a step. The Steam round trip is unchanged, the gate still fires first, and the tap count in the project rules is untouched.

It removes a loss. On a page, the case a person chose, the count they set and the price they were reading are behind them. In a dialog they are behind the scrim, still on the screen, so a person can see the thing they are signing in for while they decide whether to sign in.

The route defect above narrows from four screens to three. It does not close, and this node does not claim it does.

The map already held this type, and this node was the exception

The IA registry carries five node types and dialog is one of them. Cluster 2's other member, 2.1 the geo gate, has been a dialog since it was written, and 0.4 cookie consent is the second. 2.4 was the only node in cluster 2 typed as a page while its own flow put it on top of another screen. This is the map becoming consistent with itself rather than a new pattern entering it.

They are never on screen at once

On the case screen the first interaction fires the gate, and the sign-in dialog opens only once the gate has passed. Two layers in sequence, never stacked, and stage 04 draws them that way.

Why the address had to survive

Deleting it would have silently deleted four reasoned rules, all of them in this node's own SEO checklist: that noindex is a meta tag readable at a URL, that the route back is a crawlable anchor, that the statement is text rather than an image, and that the surface is identical for a person and a crawler. A rule stops applying by decision, not as a side effect of a carrier change.

The address is also the only carrier for a typed URL, a deep link, a session with no script, a crawler, and this node's own "already signed in" state, which exists precisely because a person can type it. One content, two carriers, and the canon drawn at stage 04 is the address.

The dialog contract, inherited and not invented

Node 0.1 fixed the modal contract for this product and this node takes it rather than writing a second one: the scrim blocks the content behind, and nothing is raised above the scrim. Three dismissals, the first renamed because there is nothing to select here: the close control, the scrim, and Escape. Dismissing records nothing.

Focus moves in on open and is trapped, and returns to the control that opened it. The surface behind is inert and is never removed, which is 0.1's own rule for a gate open and holds for the same reason: removing the carriers would make the dialog read as an ejection rather than a step.

The dialog never closes itself. Not on 2.5, not on 2.6, not on a timer. A layer that vanishes on failure returns a person to a screen that cannot explain what just happened, and section 2.5's whole subject is a failure that told a person something false.

What it costs, printed rather than smoothed

The footer leaves this surface in the dialog and stays at the address. A dialog carries no footer and the surface behind it already has one.

The dialog carries no H1. The host page already has one, and a dialog that adds a second has broken the document outline of a page it does not own. The same text is the dialog's accessible name instead, and its headings step down one level from the address's.

At 360px it is a full height sheet, not a centred card, because this node's one hard adaptive requirement is that blocks 1 and 2 are both above the fold, and a centred card at that width is a card with margins above and below it whose fold arrives sooner than on the page the requirement was written for. From the width where a centred card fits without shortening the list, it is a centred card. The block order never changes between the two carriers or between the two widths.

Four providers, and a dialog that is simple without being thinner

D-55 and D-56, 21 August 2026, taken the same day as D-54 with three competitor arrival modals beside this node.

D-55 overrides this node's own argument, and the argument stays on the page

Section 1 argued for one provider and it was not a taste argument: withdrawal is to Steam and Steam only at launch, so an account made through Google, Discord or X cannot receive what it wins until Steam is linked. That is barrier B4-1's own shape, an account that can pay in and cannot take out, built into the sign-up.

The founder overrode it knowing that, and the paragraph stays where it was, marked, because a deleted argument cannot be reread when the decision is revisited.

What makes the override honest is where the sentence sits, not that it exists. C4 generalised: what is required to withdraw is stated before the money moves. So the requirement is printed on this surface, beside the three buttons that carry it, in both carriers, and never in a settings page a person finds afterwards.

One primary and three secondary, and the difference is structural. Steam is the only provider that can receive a skin, so it is the only one drawn as the act. Four equal buttons would say the four are equal.

The order changed, in both carriers, which is why D-54's rule still holds

The two declarations now come before the provider controls. D-54 fixed that the block order never changes between the carriers, so it changed in both or in neither. This node's own default state already required the reason to be stated in text above the control, which is an order and not a decoration. And D-55 made it structural: one inert control above its own explanation was tolerable, four is a wall of dead buttons a person meets before being told why they are dead.

What was not taken from the three captures

No email field and no password field, in any state. Two of the three modals offer them beside the providers; the founder cut it and this node had already forbidden it under B6.

No single bundled checkbox. One capture ships "I'm 18+ and I agree to the Terms and Conditions" as one line. One gesture answering two questions is exactly what D-26 split, and it stays split.

No separate sign-up route. There is no separate registration in this product; the provider round trip is both, and a second door implies a first one that is different.

The statement no competitor prints

This is the block the node exists for, and its shape is fixed by the evidence rather than by preference.

The person arriving here has already been taught a test

"To open the one of the free daily cases it requires you to change your avatar to a certain thing. But now I've realised the site is a scam"

Barrier B3-2, one signal. And the community's own security rule, from B3-1's thread, quoted once:

"Log in steam community first. If any site asks for password etc though, its fake"

The page either passes that test visibly or it fails it silently. There is no third outcome.

What this page is not, and one absence that is a known orphan

It is not registration in the ordinary sense. No password field, no email field, no username, no profile step and no second provider. Hellcase's arrival modal offers three providers. This product offers one.

One route, and it follows from the exit rather than from simplicity. Withdrawal is to Steam and Steam only at launch, so an account not tied to a Steam identity cannot receive what it wins. A second provider would create accounts that can pay in and cannot take out, which is barrier B4-1's shape built into the sign-up.

There is no email anywhere on this map, and that is carried rather than fixed here

The global sweep already records it in the open: an out-of-product notification channel has no node, because it has no parent. There is no notification, email or push row anywhere in the backlog, while 5.3 and 5.8 imply a signal that reaches a person outside the page. This node does not fix that by quietly adding an email field.

When this page is reached, and the route defect it sits inside

Sign in never precedes the gate. That ordering is the whole of node 2.1.

Four screens before the activation node, and the threshold is three. Three since D-54

The flows name it: a first-time visitor touches Home, the case screen, the gate and sign in before the first reveal resolves. The product defers its first value further than the research promised, and Related Job 2 asks for the entire open-to-result experience in under 60 seconds without a confusing step, which a Steam OpenID round trip is not.

The obvious fix is already rejected on the record, so this node carries the cost rather than reopening it. A free demo reveal on identical odds and seeds was dropped in the T2 divergence, because it argues our case by demonstrating that the sceptic is right about the odds, and it spends the reveal, the one thing we sell, before anyone has decided anything.

What this node can do about it is narrow, and it is done: make the round trip predictable, so the one confusing step is not ours.

Block 5 carries a constraint it does not get to soften

C4: the amount required to withdraw is stated before the deposit and can never rise. And the residual risk travels with it, unsmoothed: the credit teaches a first-session user that opening is free, which is the one impression the rest of the map spends its budget contradicting.

Block 2 is two checkboxes and not one, and the reason is in the canon rather than in taste

The live product's version is a single line, "I'm 18+ and I agree to the Terms and Conditions", with no required attribute and with the provider buttons not disabled while it is unchecked, baseline behind the account.

One checkbox bundles a contract consent with an age declaration, and a person who ticks it to get past it has made one gesture that answered two questions. Splitting them costs one line and makes the age declaration a separate deliberate act, which is the only property that makes it worth anything at all.

And block 2 does not claim to be verification. It is a self-declaration, the surface says so, and 2.7 is where the other layer lives. D-26 part 2 proposes taking 2.7 out of round 1 and prints what that costs; until that go is given this node assumes 2.7 still exists.

No block for: a password field, an email field, a second provider, a case image, a ticker, a countdown, or a bonus larger than the one 1.0 already promised.

Three states added to the map, named rather than smuggled in

Returning from Steam, cancelled at Steam, already signed in. None is a numbered node, and each is declared because the map fixed a specific list of five internal loading states and these sit outside it.

Cancelled and refused are different events and the map holds only the second. The flows draw two branches out of the round trip, refused and down. A deliberate cancel is neither: nothing failed. Folding it into 2.5 would tell a person who changed their mind that something went wrong.

The failure told the person something false about themselves. That is the defect, not the failure.

The rule that outranks the copy

No password field appears in this state, or in any state of this page. A failed third-party sign in followed by a password form is the exact shape the community warns about in B3-1's own thread, and it is B6's rule. A retry re-runs the OpenID round trip. It never asks for a credential.

Retry is allowed here, and that is the opposite of 2.3

A failed sign in is a fact about an identity provider, so trying again is a legitimate second attempt at the same question. An under-age declaration at 2.3 is a statement the person made about themselves, so re-asking it is coaching rather than a retry. The two states look similar and behave in opposite directions, and that is written down so stage 04 does not build one component for both.

The one thing it must never be

A spinner. A person waiting on an indefinite loader against a dead provider has been given a failure with the failure removed, and this is the product whose first principle is that its claims are checkable.
