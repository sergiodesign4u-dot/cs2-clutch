B1. The Steam trade URL, and why this node exists at all

Withdrawal to Steam is round 1 and it works by sending a trade offer. A trade offer needs a trade URL, which carries a token the person gets from Steam and gives to us. No node on the map holds that field. withdrawal.md names three limits before the request, blocked countries, Steam trade holds and Steam-side bans, and it never names the one precondition the person themselves controls.

So the exit has a missing input and the map did not know. That is the whole justification for this page in round 1, and it is a better one than the row in the account menu that put it there.

Two homes, and it is not a duplicate. 5.3 states the requirement before the request, which is row G5's own rule: the person meets the limit before the first withdrawal rather than inside it. This node is where the field is set and changed, outside the flow, on a day when nobody is withdrawing anything. That is the same shape responsible play already has in three carriers.

What is refused inside this block. No masking of the saved value, because a person needs to compare it with the one Steam shows them. No silent trimming of what was pasted. And no acceptance of a value we have not checked: a field that saves anything and fails at the exit is the rising-threshold defect with a text input in front of it.

Refusing a value is a state, not an error message

A trade URL is either one Steam will accept or it is not, and we can tell before the exit. So the saving act checks it, and a value that does not check is refused at the moment it is offered with the cause in words, B5.

What it may never do: accept the value, look saved, and fail three days later inside a withdrawal. That is barrier B8-3's shape, a requirement that arrives after the commitment, and C4 already fixed the general rule that what is required to withdraw is stated before the money moves.

What this page refuses, and each refusal has a live parent

No preference this product cannot honour

Nothing here configures a channel, a mode or a surface that does not exist. A toggle over nothing is the dead item defect with a switch on it, and eleven of the baseline's twenty rows are exactly that for us.

No switch that turns off a confirmation before money moves

The baseline has two whose subjects are live for us, confirmation on selling a skin back and confirmation on withdrawing one. Selling back is round 1 since D-38 and withdrawal is round 1, so scope does not refuse these.

Design principle 3 does, quoted: "Where money is about to be spent, odds, cost and expected value are visible and legible. Cost never hides inside excitement." A switch that removes the confirmation before a value leaves the account is a switch that removes a risk moment, and it is the one preference in this list a person sets once, in a calm minute, and meets months later at the worst one.

The cost is printed rather than absorbed. Somebody who withdraws every day will confirm every day, and that is friction we are choosing. The answer to it is a faster confirmation, not an absent one.

No limit that acquires completion mechanics, and no second home for anything

CLAUDE.md, quoted: "A limit never acquires completion mechanics, streaks, status or a session score." Nothing on this page counts anything, and this is the page it would be easiest to break that rule on, because a settings page is where a profile completeness meter goes.

Sound, language, the break tool and sign out are all somewhere already. They appear here as routes and never as controls. Superseded by D-89 and D-136: sound, language and sign out are controls here too, and sound and language are one setting each, read and written by both carriers, so there is no second value to drift.
