The country control, and refusing it is a compliance decision rather than a scope one

Your Selected Country: United States, with a CHANGE button. baseline-account.md section 7.2 walked it and stated what it is: together with the terms clause "services exclusively to residents of countries where such activities are not prohibited", this is the entire market control of the live product. The person declares their own country and can change it at any time.

D-23 chose the opposite and this node is where the distance is paid. The allowlist is closed by default, a market with no row is not launched rather than open, and 2.1 is designed around that state. A self-declared country the person can change is the geo control handed to the person being controlled, which is not a stricter version of our rule, it is the absence of one.

And the baseline demonstrates the failure mode on itself: two self-declared countries in one account, and they disagree, because the deposit step carries its own country field beside this one. A product with two answers to the same question has none.

Three blocks, and one of them is a list of things that are not here

That is unusual and it is deliberate: this page's baseline has twenty rows and ours has one, so a person arriving from a product where sound, language and the break tool are all in settings will look for them here.

B1. The Steam trade URL, and why this node exists at all

Withdrawal to Steam is round 1 and it works by sending a trade offer. A trade offer needs a trade URL, which carries a token the person gets from Steam and gives to us. No node on the map holds that field. withdrawal.md names three limits before the request, blocked countries, Steam trade holds and Steam-side bans, and it never names the one precondition the person themselves controls.

So the exit has a missing input and the map did not know. That is the whole justification for this page in round 1, and it is a better one than the row in the account menu that put it there.

Two homes, and it is not a duplicate. 5.3 states the requirement before the request, which is row G5's own rule: the person meets the limit before the first withdrawal rather than inside it. This node is where the field is set and changed, outside the flow, on a day when nobody is withdrawing anything. That is the same shape responsible play already has in three carriers.

What is refused inside this block. No masking of the saved value, because a person needs to compare it with the one Steam shows them. No silent trimming of what was pasted. And no acceptance of a value we have not checked: a field that saves anything and fails at the exit is the rising-threshold defect with a text input in front of it.

B2. Make me anonymous, stated and not drawn

public-result.md has carried this open item since 21 August 2026 in its own words: "What remains open is whether they can turn it off, which no node says." Owner: the founder.

The baseline answers where such a control would live and does not answer whether ours has one. Make me Anonymous sits in the SECURITY group of this screen, and if we ship one this is where it goes, because nowhere else on the map is a place a person manages what is true about their own account.

Why this node will not decide it. The toggle changes what 0.8 renders in every tile and what 7.1 renders in block 6, and D-69 already left two of our own decisions pointing opposite ways about a public profile. A control that rewrites two published nodes is not a settings row, it is a decision with a settings row attached.

And the cost of leaving it out is printed rather than absorbed: until it is decided, the product publishes a person's display name on every win in the live feed and on every shared result, and that person has no way to stop it.

B3. Where the rest of it lives, as routes rather than as controls

Sound and language at the foot of the rail, the break tool on 6.1, sign out in the account menu, items on 5.1, rolls on 5.9, the account's own record on 5.10, the documents on 0.9, the cookie answer through the footer control that 0.4 reopens.

This block is a route list and never a control list. A control here would make this page the second home for something that already has one, which is the defect the whole node is built to avoid.

Refusing a value is a state, not an error message

A trade URL is either one Steam will accept or it is not, and we can tell before the exit. So the saving act checks it, and a value that does not check is refused at the moment it is offered with the cause in words, B5.

What it may never do: accept the value, look saved, and fail three days later inside a withdrawal. That is barrier B8-3's shape, a requirement that arrives after the commitment, and C4 already fixed the general rule that what is required to withdraw is stated before the money moves.

What this page refuses, and each refusal has a live parent

No preference this product cannot honour

Nothing here configures a channel, a mode or a surface that does not exist. A toggle over nothing is the dead item defect with a switch on it, and eleven of the baseline's twenty rows are exactly that for us.

No switch that turns off a confirmation before money moves

The baseline has two whose subjects are live for us, confirmation on selling a skin back and confirmation on withdrawing one. Selling back is round 1 since D-38 and withdrawal is round 1, so scope does not refuse these.

Design principle 3 does, quoted: "Where money is about to be spent, odds, cost and expected value are visible and legible. Cost never hides inside excitement." A switch that removes the confirmation before a value leaves the account is a switch that removes a risk moment, and it is the one preference in this list a person sets once, in a calm minute, and meets months later at the worst one.

The cost is printed rather than absorbed. Somebody who withdraws every day will confirm every day, and that is friction we are choosing. The answer to it is a faster confirmation, not an absent one.

No limit that acquires completion mechanics, and no second home for anything

CLAUDE.md, quoted: "A limit never acquires completion mechanics, streaks, status or a session score." Nothing on this page counts anything, and this is the page it would be easiest to break that rule on, because a settings page is where a profile completeness meter goes.

Sound, language, the break tool and sign out are all somewhere already. They appear here as routes and never as controls.

SEO, indexation, 360px and access

Not indexed, and it owes 0.13 a row it does not have

No canonical, no schema, no breadcrumb, and a noindex header. A private account surface, and the register treats the whole 5.x group that way. URL /settings, no language prefix, one destination carrying one label in every carrier.

D-78 already recorded that the register has no row for 5.9, 5.10 or 5.11, and its closing count of nine indexed against six not indexed was taken before those three nodes existed. This node repeats that finding rather than editing another node's register.

One column at every width, and never two

At 360: the field at full width with the helper line under it, then the stated absence, then the route list stacked. The saved value wraps rather than truncating at the end, because a trade URL a person cannot read in full is one they cannot compare with the one Steam shows them.

From 900: the same single column at a readable measure. The baseline runs two columns and that is what lets twenty rows look like a small page. Ours has one block and a list, and putting them side by side would be a dashboard made of nothing.

  • Exactly one H1, and it is the page name
  • A real input with a real label, never a placeholder doing the label's job: a placeholder disappears the moment somebody types, which is the moment they most need to know what the field is
  • The helper line is beside the field and not behind an icon. A link a person needs in order to complete the field is part of the field
  • The refusal message is text, in the same block, associated with the input, and never colour alone
  • Every target at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, matching 0.1's AAA choice
  • Nothing here saves without a press. An account setting that commits on blur is one a person changes by scrolling past it
