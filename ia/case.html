Why the numbers sit above the table rather than under it

Blocks 4 and 5 are two figures and three figures. Block 6 is the whole inventory of the case and at 360px it is the longest thing on the page. Putting the summary under the list means the cost of the case is read after its contents, which is design principle 3 failing on layout rather than on content.

And why the ticker is at position 8. The baseline puts its ticker under the header, and on 1.0 that placement is 1.0's to keep. Here the top of the page is the commit block. Pattern E is a layer on the screen where the core happens, not the event itself, and a strip of other people's wins directly beside this product's own spend trigger is the ambient layer competing with the thing it is ambient to.

The one thing the baseline already does, and what it changes

baseline.md records the finding in its own words: D2 is not a new capability, it is an inherited one. The baseline publishes chance, price and ticket range per item, pre-login, on every card. What it publishes the price in is coins, and C1 deletes that denomination entirely, so the pairing survives the migration only if the value is denominated in real currency. The real distance between the baseline and this map is D3, D4 and a provably fair page a logged-out person can open, not D2.

Why the pairing is the point rather than either number, from the bank verbatim: a chance without a value is a lottery ticket with no prize printed on it, and a value without a chance is a shop window.

The freshness stamp, and the door it comes attached to

skin.club stamps its odds panel "UPDATED 11 MINUTES AGO" and puts the panel behind a control. 0.11 rule 2 requires the stamp on anything that moves, so the stamp is taken and the door is refused. Their freshness discipline is real, and their default is to hide the number the person came for.

What the stamp covers, precisely. One stamp on the drop table, covering the value column, which is the per-read figure. The published chance is static until the configuration changes and carries no stamp. Two figures on one card with different refresh classes must not share one as-of, because a stamp that covers both quietly claims the chance was re-read too.

D3. The observed rate counter, and the condition travels with it

Conditional on D-B, and on D-C in common with every published figure on this map. Both conditions are printed here rather than in a footnote, because this is the block that does not ship if either comes back negative.

Published, observed, N. The observed figure is the outcome count from the same settlement ledger that pays users. N is printed always, and printed alone below the sample floor, because a percentage over a handful of rolls is not a smaller truth, it is a different claim.

Never reset, and a configuration change forks it: the old configuration keeps its history and stays visible, the new one starts at zero and says so.

The honest limit. At launch this row is infrastructure and it does not close B7-2 until N is large enough to say so. Until D-B returns, that barrier is answered by D2 and D4 and by nothing else, which the map records as a materially weaker answer rather than assuming it away.

It is not a personal figure and never becomes one

Personal streak math was dropped in the same divergence that chose this row: correct mathematics delivered next to a spend button to a losing user is loss-chasing encouragement and the single easiest artefact to hold up in a hearing. Nothing on this page compares a person's own results to the published distribution, in any phase, including the outcome.

And it is where the round proof stops. Node 0.14 sends the weight-table question here by name. The proof block proves the roll was fixed before the click. This block is the other question, and the two are never merged into one claim.

0.11 calls the EV row the strongest in the register, because a reader who distrusts the figure can derive it without leaving the page. That property is a layout requirement rather than a nicety: the EV figure and the drop table have to be on one page, which they are, and the values in the table have to be the values the EV was computed from, which is a production rule this node states and stage 04 carries.

A named comparison is allowed and bounded. Hellcase 82.1 percent and DatDrop 80.6 percent across 118 cases are tested figures; Key-Drop 89 percent and Clash.gg 90 to 92.8 percent are stated ones. A comparison never mixes the two, and if only a stated figure exists for a competitor, no comparison is printed at all.

The drop table is a comparison table, and it has an order for a reason

Built as a table, not as a decorated carousel. Grouped by tier, ordered by ticket range.

Grouped by rarity tier. The bank walked the ladder live on two Hellcase case pages: seven tiers, and the seventh appears only on a case that contains knives or gloves. Grouping is not decoration here: D3 puts the observed rate counter "beside every published tier percentage", so the tier is the unit the counter attaches to, and a table with no tier grouping has nowhere to put block 5.

Ordered by ticket range inside each tier, and that is the default. The ticket range is the interval the roll resolves against. An order a person can reconstruct is an order they can check, which is the same argument node 0.14 makes about the hash. Sorting by chance or by value is a control the person may apply, and every row keeps its ticket range, so the checkable order is never lost.

The contradiction this state exposes, and it is not resolvable inside this node

Three backlog rows are not simultaneously satisfiable as written, and nothing in this repository has said so. D1 says the roll can only select items with at least one free unit. D2 says a published chance is printed per item. D3 says a weights change forks the counter. So what is the published chance of the other items while this one is at zero?

ReadingWhat it meansWhat it costs
A. Recomputed over the selectable entriesThe table's percentages move whenever stock moves, and the person always reads the chance actually usedEvery stock change is a weights change, so D3's fork rule fires, and at any real volume the observed counter forks itself into uselessness. D1 and D3 cancel
B. The configuration's chance, with the zero item unreachableThe table is stable and the counter accumulatesThe printed number is not the number used, which is barrier B7-2 printed in our own drop table. This is the thing the product exists not to do

Recommendation: C for a small backed catalogue, A with a stated recomputation for a large one, and B never. The choice is not a design decision. It is a settlement and treasury decision that changes the meaning of two published numbers, so it goes to the open table with an owner, and stage 04 draws the card the same way under all three.

This is what a state node is for. Without 3.4 this question arrives at implementation as a bug report about a percentage that moved.

The three rules that make the buildup honest

The reveal renders and never computes, E1. The roll is settled before the animation begins, and node 0.14 carries the implementation half: recomputation is verification of a settled fact, and the two must not share a code path. Divergence between the animation and the credit is impossible by construction.

No near-miss theatre, E2. The deceleration lands on the actual result without visually passing better items first. A near-miss pre-calculated as a loss is fabricated tension, named as a confirmed dark pattern in the research and refused in the jobs file.

The hash reframes the wait, and that is the design claim E4 actually makes. The animation is honest theatre over a pre-committed result. The chip is what makes the buildup legible as watching a settled outcome rather than waiting for one to be decided.

Timing, and the two figures the research gives

research.md section 6 carries both, and this node prints both rather than picking one silently: "4-6 seconds for single-case (full ritual)" in the duration calibration line, and "the 3-6 seconds of genuine tension during the spin are the emotional core of the product" in the Pattern B rejection.

Taken here: 4 to 6 seconds for a single open, the figure from the line that is about calibration. The discrepancy is small and it is recorded rather than smoothed, because stage 04 will time an animation against one of these numbers.

Same mechanism, opposite reason. The reduced-motion path shows the same settled outcome, in the same order, with the animation stilled or shortened, and it never changes the cost, the odds or the result.

Audio, and the open item it answers. E3 makes audio one of the four channels that scale with rarity. Node 0.1 carries a sound control with a stated open item: it has no parent in any of the three legal classes, and "it ships with a stated parent or it does not ship". The parent it was missing is on this page: the reveal has audio because E3 requires it, and a person needs to be able to stop it. That is an argument this node can supply and cannot ratify, so it goes to 0.1's owner as a candidate parent rather than as a decision taken here.

The trigger does not re-arm. There is no second Open control on screen during a reveal and no queued open. That is the same rule that refuses the multi-open selector, applied to time instead of to quantity.

The receipt is priced as an instance, and that is the whole of F1

The pain is not that a number was optimistic. It is that the number described a different object than the one received, a name instead of an instance. We hold the exact asset before awarding it, so its float, paint seed and phase are known, and it is priced as that unit.

Two hard dependencies travel with it. It is denominated in real currency, because the dropped rival left that rule attached to the pick: a receipt in coins fails its own test. And the venue link is filtered to the float band, so the person checks it themselves rather than taking our word. Which two venues is [?], owner founder, before stage 04.

And it persists, which is row F2. The receipt stays attached to the item in inventory 5.1 and on the withdrawal record 5.3. The success signal is that the same receipt is retrievable a week later. This node does not own those surfaces, it owns the obligation that what it renders here is the same object they render later.

C. SEO text, finished copy

How this case works. The entry cost buys one roll. The roll lands on one item, and every item's share of the possible outcomes is printed on its card as a percentage and as a ticket range. The two say the same thing twice: the percentage is easy to read, and the ticket range is the interval the roll actually resolves against, which is what makes a result checkable afterwards.

How a chance and a ticket range fit together. A roll produces a number. That number falls inside exactly one ticket range, and the item holding that range is the item you get. The round is fixed before the animation starts, and the hash of it is on screen at the moment you open. After the reveal, one link checks that round against the number that was published before it ran.

What happens when an item runs out. Every item in this case points at a unit we hold. When the last unit of an item is won, the item stays on this page showing zero free units and cannot be rolled. It is not removed, because a table that quietly loses rows is a table you cannot check against what you read a minute ago.

This block stays the property of this node. Stage 05 aligns it to voice and syncs the final version back here. Interface strings do not move into it.

D. Structured data

Product plus Offer. The case is the thing bought and the entry cost is its price.

The refusal, inherited and restated because this is the node it constrains. The drop table items are not offers. We do not sell that skin at that price, and marking each item as a Product with an Offer would state that we do. The items are content of the case, and the only Offer on this page is the entry cost.

BreadcrumbList on the visible breadcrumb. No aggregateRating and no Review anywhere.

E. Optimisation checklist

  1. Exactly one H1, and it is the case name.
  2. H2s exist in block order, matching block B.
  3. Breadcrumb drawn and marked up.
  4. Every figure is crawlable text, none is an image. The pre-login figures on this page are the product's entire pre-login argument, and an image would remove them from search and from a screen reader in the same stroke.
