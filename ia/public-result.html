The gap this node closes

Nothing in this repository said what leaves the platform when a person shares a win. F4 is the single exception to the priority test in the whole backlog, the path does not break without it but the referral loop does, and a capability that emits an object into the world with no definition of what that object is, is a gap.

This node is the definition. One open, one page: the skin instance that was won, the case it came from, and the round proof that settles it, all readable by somebody with no account, from a link they were sent.

Two jobs, and the second one was found late

Emotional Job 2, a win worth talking about, jtbd.md:93, closed by row F4. This is the parent D-20 acted on.

Related Job 3, verify the outcome after I open, jtbd.md:51. Not a tick placed to rescue a screen: the job's own outcome clause reads "so that I trust the platform enough to open again or to tell someone about my result". That line had been in jtbd.md since stage 02 and nobody had traced it.

The primary action on this page is a verification, not a purchase

The most-shared surface the product owns leads with "check this round yourself" rather than with "open this case". That is design principle 1 arriving at the one place where it costs revenue, and it is the reason this page is worth building rather than pointing the share at a landing page.

The share affordance F4, and what publication actually means here

The thing this node found, and it is a real one.

F4 reads as though sharing is what makes a result public. The ticker says otherwise

0.8 says every ticker tile lands on 7.1, and a ticker tile is a win the person did not share. So for any open the ticker carries, the page exists before anybody taps anything, and F4 hands the person a link to an object that is already public.

Both readings were live in the map at once and neither was written down. The node is specified as the superset, which is the treatment 2.7 got for the same reason: one branch is deleted later rather than the whole node redrawn.

One rule applied three times

No value figure of any kind. An unfurl image is frozen at render time and cannot carry an as-of, and 0.11 rule 2 requires one on anything that moves. A price baked into a card rots silently while still reading as current, and that is exactly what B7-1 describes: a number that describes a different object than the one received.

No multiplier and no boast, per 0.11 rule 7 and the survivorship refusal above. No verified mark, per 0.14 section 0, and an image cannot be checked, which makes it the worst possible carrier for a proof claim.

The cost of this rule, stated rather than hidden

The story a person wants to tell has a number in it, and the card refuses to carry the number. The card is weaker for it. The page is one tap away and carries the receipt with its as-of, in text, where it can be read, copied and checked. A card that travelled further by carrying a figure nobody can check would be trading design principle 1 for a click.

Node 7.2. Result gone or private

State. MVP. Three ways to arrive: the owner revoked it, the account is closed or restricted, or the id never existed at all.

One response for all three, and it is a privacy decision rather than a shortcut

Distinguishing "revoked" from "never existed" tells a stranger whether a particular person ever had a particular result, which is precisely what a revoke was meant to stop telling them. So the three arrivals render one state with one message.

HTTP 404, not 410 and not 200. 410 Gone states that the resource existed, which leaks the same fact the message refuses to leak. 200 would be a soft 404, which 0.3 forbids outright. The page is noindex either way, so nothing here is an indexation trick.

The limit, named rather than glossed

It routes into 1.2, where the round can still be checked without this page. That is 0.14 state seven, inherited, and it is right: a round proof does not depend on this page existing.

C. The strings that travel

One open, and everything behind it.

This is a single result: the item, what it was worth at the moment it was won, the case it came from, and the round that produced it.

The proof is on this page and it needs no account. The server seed hash was published before the roll. Take the fields, recompute them yourself, with our tool or with anyone's.

What that proves: this round's outcome was fixed before the click and was not altered afterwards. What it does not prove: that the published chances are the chances used. That question has a different answer, and it is the observed rate beside every published percentage.

Stage 05 aligns this to voice and syncs the final version back into block C of the md.

D and E. Structured data, and the checklist

None, per 0.13 section 3. The reason is worth carrying: the two refusals in its section 7 are the same rule twice, markup describes what a page truly is, and there is no schema type that truthfully describes one settled roll. The skin is not an Offer, because we do not sell that skin at that price, and nothing here is a Review or an aggregateRating.

One H1. LCP is the skin image, so it is the one image on this page that is preloaded and sized. Every route a crawlable <a>, including the two routes out, because follow is doing work here. Canonical self, no hreflang. The proof is text, never an image: a hash in a picture cannot be pasted into anyone's verifier, which is the point of the whole node.

Accessibility. The unfurl image carries alt text and the page never depends on the image to state anything. Both value figures are programmatically paired with their labels and their as-of stamps, so a screen reader never reads a number with no idea what it is or when it was true. The accessible name of the hash field is the field, not the value. The owner's controls are real controls, and revoke is not a hover affordance.
