# Node 5.11. Settings

**Type:** page. **Group:** `account`. **Scope:** MVP. **Cluster:** 5, take out what I earned.

**Purpose.** Hold the one thing a person has to set about their own account that is not held anywhere else on the map, and say plainly where everything else lives. **In round 1 that is one field: the Steam trade URL, without which the exit does not work.**

**Jobs served.** None directly. **Parent class: none in the three legal classes, and the cell is printed rather than filled.** `5.11` arrived on the map by founder decision `D-36` of 20 August 2026, together with `5.10`, so that the account menu could carry live rows instead of dead ones. That is a legitimate reason and it is not a barrier code, a job or a named compliance constraint.

**But the one block it holds does have a parent, and that is the difference between this node and `5.10`.** The Steam trade URL is not a new capability: it is the precondition of a capability that already has one. Withdrawal to Steam is round 1, rows `G1` and `G5` at `cjm-to-be.md`, on barriers `B8-2` and `B8-3`. **An orphan may not grow, `D-78`, and this is not growth: it is a field a live row already requires and no node holds.**

**Entered from:** the account menu on `0.1`, row 5 of seven. Also from `5.3` when the exit cannot proceed, section 3.

**Transitions:** `5.3` withdrawal, `6.1` responsible play, `0.10` support.

**Baseline row, `5.11`.** The rule in `CLAUDE.md`: every node carries one baseline row, what the live product does, what we keep, what we change and why.

| What the live product does | What we keep | What we change, and why |
|---|---|---|
| A two column settings tab with **twenty rows** in four groups: `GENERAL` five, `SECURITY` three, `LINKED PROFILES` four, `NOTIFICATIONS` eight. ### 1.3 `LINKED PROFILES`, four rows, and they are another node's unbuilt debt

**Steam shown linked, then three unlinked.** Round 1 has four sign-in providers since `D-55`, and only one of them can receive a skin.

**This is not a block for this node and it is not nothing either.** `D-55` printed three states as debt on 21 August 2026 and none of them is built: **no Steam linked** on `5.3`, **Link Steam** on `5.1`, and somebody with no Steam linked about to spend on `4.1`. **This capture adds a fourth surface that debt reaches**, because the one field this node holds cannot be filled by an account that has no Steam identity at all. Section 3.4 draws that state and section 7 sends the finding back to `D-55`'s three owners rather than absorbing it here.

### 1.4 The country control, and why refusing it is a compliance decision rather than a scope one

**`Your Selected Country: United States`, with a `CHANGE` button.** `baseline-account.md` section 7.2 walked it and stated what it is: together with the terms clause "services exclusively to residents of countries where such activities are not prohibited", **this is the entire market control of the live product. The person declares their own country and can change it at any time.**

**`D-23` chose the opposite and this node is where the distance is paid.** The allowlist is closed by default, a market with no row is `not launched` rather than open, and `2.1` is designed around that state. **A self-declared country that the person can change is the geo control handed to the person being controlled**, which is not a stricter version of our rule, it is the absence of one.

**And the baseline already demonstrates the failure mode on itself.** `baseline-account.md` section 5b.6: **two self-declared countries in one account, and they disagree**, because the deposit step carries its own country field beside this one. A product with two answers to the same question has none.

**Quoted parent:** the compliance constraint in `CLAUDE.md`, "a market allowlist closed by default, so a market with no row is `not launched` rather than open and that is the state the geo layer is designed around, `D-23`".

## 2. The blocks

| Block | Parent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **B1. The Steam trade URL** | Rows `G1` and `G5` at `cjm-to-be.md`, on barriers `B8-2` and `B8-3`, by way of node `5.3` | Section 2.1 |

**Three blocks, and one of them is a list of things that are not here.** That is unusual and it is deliberate: this page's baseline has twenty rows and ours has one, so a person arriving from a product where sound, language and the break tool are all in settings will look for them here.

### 2.1 The trade URL, and why this node exists at all

**Withdrawal to Steam is round 1 and it works by sending a trade offer.** A trade offer needs a trade URL, which carries a token the person gets from Steam and gives to us. **No node on the map holds that field.** `withdrawal.md` names three limits before the request, blocked countries, Steam trade holds and Steam-side bans, and it never names the one precondition the person themselves controls.

**So the exit has a missing input and the map did not know.** That is the whole justification for this page in round 1, and it is a better one than the row in the account menu that put it there.

**Two homes, and it is not a duplicate.** `5.3` states the requirement **before the request**, which is its own rule, row `G5`: the person meets the limit before the first withdrawal rather than inside it. **This node is where the field is set and changed**, outside the flow, on a day when nobody is withdrawing anything. **That is the same shape responsible play already has in three carriers**, and `navigation.md` states the principle: each answers a different moment.

**The helper line is inherited.** The baseline prints "Here you can get your trade URL" with a link, because the token is not something a person can invent. Ours does the same and the destination is Steam's own page.

**What is refused inside this block.** No masking of the saved value, because a person needs to compare it with the one Steam shows them. No silent trimming or rewriting of what was pasted. **And no acceptance of a value we have not checked**, section 3.3: a field that saves anything and fails at the exit is the rising-threshold defect with a text input in front of it.

### 2.2 Where the rest of it lives, as routes rather than as controls

**A list of destinations, not a second set of switches.** Sound and language at the foot of the rail, the break tool on `6.1`, sign out in the account menu, items on `5.1`, rolls on `5.9`, the account's own record on `5.10`, the documents on `0.9`, the cookie answer through the footer control that `0.4` reopens.

**This block is a route list and never a control list.** A control here would make this page the second home for something that already has one, which is the defect the whole node is built to avoid.

### 3.2 The anonymity block is stated and not drawn, and the reason is in another node

`public-result.md` carries this open item in its own words, and it has carried it since 21 August 2026: **"What remains open is whether they can turn it off, which no node says."** Owner: the founder.

**The baseline answers where such a control would live and it does not answer whether ours has one.** `Make me Anonymous` sits in the `SECURITY` group of this screen, `OFF` / `ON`, and if we ship one this is where it goes, because nowhere else on the map is a place a person manages what is true about their own account.

**Why this node will not decide it.** The toggle changes what `0.8` renders in every tile and what `7.1` renders in block 6, and `D-69` already left two of our own decisions pointing opposite ways about a public profile. **A control that rewrites two published nodes is not a settings row, it is a decision with a settings row attached.**

**And the cost of leaving it out is printed rather than absorbed:** until it is decided, the product publishes a person's display name on every win in the live feed and on every shared result, and that person has no way to stop it. `public-result.md` already names that as a publication decision made by omission.

### 3.3 Refusing a value is a state, not an error message

**A trade URL is either one Steam will accept or it is not, and we can tell before the exit.** So the saving act checks it, and a value that does not check is refused **at the moment it is offered** with the cause in words, `B5`.

**What it may never do:** accept the value, look saved, and fail three days later inside a withdrawal. That is barrier `B8-3`'s shape, a requirement that arrives after the commitment, and `C4` already fixed the general rule that what is required to withdraw is stated before the money moves.

## 4. What this page refuses, and each refusal has a live parent

### 4.1 No preference this product cannot honour

Nothing here configures a channel, a mode or a surface that does not exist. **A toggle over nothing is the dead item defect with a switch on it**, and eleven of the baseline's twenty rows are exactly that for us: they configure case battles, gunfights, upgrades, crypto withdrawal, giveaways, a partner programme, and notification channels with no backlog row.

### 4.2 No switch that turns off a confirmation before money moves

**The baseline has two whose subjects are live for us**, confirmation on selling a skin back and confirmation on withdrawing one. Selling back is round 1 since `D-38` and withdrawal is round 1, so scope does not refuse these.

**Design principle 3 does, quoted:** "Where money is about to be spent, odds, cost and expected value are visible and legible. Cost never hides inside excitement." **A switch that removes the confirmation before a value leaves the account is a switch that removes a risk moment**, and it is the one preference in this list a person sets once, in a calm minute, and meets months later at the worst one.

**The cost is printed rather than absorbed.** Somebody who withdraws every day will confirm every day, and that is friction we are choosing. **The answer to it is a faster confirmation, not an absent one.**

### 4.3 No limit that acquires completion mechanics

`CLAUDE.md`, quoted: "A limit never acquires completion mechanics, streaks, status or a session score." **Nothing on this page counts anything**, and that is the rule this page would be the easiest place in the product to break, because a settings page is where a profile completeness meter goes.

### 4.4 No second home for anything

Sound, language, the break tool and sign out are all somewhere already. **They appear here as routes and never as controls**, section 2.2.

## 6. Adaptive behaviour and accessibility

**Mobile, base, 360px.** One column. The trade URL field at full width with the helper line under it, then the stated absence, then the route list as a stacked list. **The saved value wraps rather than truncating at the end**, because a trade URL a person cannot read in full is a trade URL they cannot compare with the one Steam shows them.

**Desktop, from 900px.** The same single column at a readable measure. **Never two columns.** The baseline runs two and it is what lets twenty rows look like a small page; ours has one block and a list, and putting them side by side would be a dashboard made of nothing.

- **Exactly one H1**, and it is the page name.
- **The field is a real `<input>` with a real `<label>`**, never a placeholder doing the label's job: a placeholder disappears the moment somebody types, which is the moment they most need to know what the field is.
- **The helper line is beside the field and not behind an icon.** A link a person needs in order to complete the field is part of the field.
- **The refusal message is text, in the same block, associated with the input**, and never colour alone.
- **Every target at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels**, matching `0.1`'s AAA choice.
- **Nothing on this page saves without a press.** An account setting that commits on blur is one a person changes by scrolling past it.
