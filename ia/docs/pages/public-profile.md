# Node 7.3. Public profile

**Type:** page. **Group:** `pages`. **Scope:** MVP. **Cluster:** 7, tell someone.

**Purpose.** Carry what one account has won into a stranger's browser, with the round behind every line of it, and require no account to read or to check any of it.

**Created 23 August 2026 by founder decision, `D-90`, and it is built against this project's own argument.** The node that refused it, `7.1` block 6, is not amended into agreement and its reason is not deleted. Read section 1 before anything else on this page.

**Parent class: none, and the cell is printed rather than filled.** Not a barrier from the As-Is map, not a job in `jtbd.md`, not a compliance constraint or a design principle. The founder asked for it directly, which is the same ground `D-36` put `5.10` and `5.11` on, and the treatment is `D-38`'s: **the capability ships with the empty parent shown and no backlog row is retro-fitted into `cjm-to-be.md`.**

**One thing it does close, and it is a routing debt rather than a parent.** `D-59` gave the live feed's avatar a public profile as its destination and printed `public profile: no node yet` on every tile of every page for two days. That mark is now off, because its subject is fixed. **A mark left standing after its subject is fixed is the next false statement.**

**URL:** `/u/<public-id>`. **Indexed:** no, `noindex, follow`. **Canonical:** self. **Schema:** none. **Breadcrumb:** none.

**Renders:** `0.6` the canonical skin card, in a stranger's field set.

**Sitemap transitions:** `7.1`, `1.2`, `1.0`.

**States specified inside this node:** the owner reading their own page, nothing won yet, and no page to show.

## 0.2 Amended 24 August 2026 by `D-93`. The page can be switched off, and it is on by default

**Founder decision, 24 August 2026.** A person can hide their public profile. The default is visible.

**Why the node needed this and did not have it.** `D-90` published a page carrying a person's name and everything they have won, and gave them no way to take it down. **That is the one shape of this node nobody argued for.** `0.8` wanted the destination, `7.1` refused a trophy shelf outright, and **neither of them wanted a permanent one**. A page about someone that they cannot withdraw is a stronger claim than either carrier ever made.

**Default visible, and the default is the decision rather than an absence of one.** Off by default would switch off something the product already does, and would empty `0.8`'s destination, this node and the route from `5.10` at once. The smaller move is that the page is on and the person it is about can switch it off.

### Exactly three things the switch changes

| Surface | Visible | Hidden |
|---|---|---|
| This address, to a stranger | The page | `player-gone.html`, the same screen an account that never existed gets |
| `0.8`, the winner's name in the strip | Name, linked here | Name, **not linked**, and no badge |
| `5.10`, "See yourself as a stranger does" | The public view | The hidden state, carrying the switch's route |

### And two things it does not change, printed on the page rather than only here

**The public page of a round already shared stays up.** `7.1` is a page about a round, its address was given out by the person who shared it, and retracting it is a different capability with a different owner. **The name still appears in the live drop strip.**

**A switch trusted for more than it does is worse than no switch**, so both exclusions render on the hidden state and on the settings row itself.

### A stranger is never told that a page is hidden

They get `player-gone.html`, and it is the same page an account that never existed gets. **A "this person hid their profile" screen publishes the very fact the setting exists to withhold**, and it tells a stranger that the address is worth trying again later. **The two causes are indistinguishable from outside, which is what makes the switch worth anything.**

**The strip carries no hidden badge either**, for the same reason and one more: a badge would mark out which accounts have something worth looking at.

**Nothing on `player-gone.html` changes for this.** Its copy already says what is true of both causes and does not speculate about which. That restraint was the right call before it had a second cause and is load bearing now that it has one.

### The hidden state shows the page, not a stub

**What a person needs in order to decide whether to turn this back on is the thing itself**, not a description of it. A hidden state that showed a stub would make the switch a guess. Same argument `D-89` made for `5.10`'s "See yourself as a stranger does": three sentences about a public footprint are three sentences taken on trust.

**The control is not on this page.** It lives once, on `5.11` under Security, and this page routes to it. One setting, one control, `D-58`.

## 1. The argument this node lost, kept whole

`D-69` left the product holding two opposite verdicts on one object for two days.

| Carrier | Verdict on a public profile |
|---|---|
| `0.8`, the live drop feed | **Has one.** Every tile's avatar pointed at it, with the absence printed under the name |
| `7.1`, block 6 | **Refuses one**, in these words: adding it "would rebuild the trophy shelf this node was created to replace" |

**The founder chose the first.** `7.1`'s refusal stands where it was written, with its reason. It lost; it did not turn out to be wrong.

**And the refusal was accurate about the object.** `baseline.md` section 9.9, the founder's capture of 21 August 2026: the live product's profile is an inventory history with four tabs, every item that account has won, priced, and nothing on it a reader can check. **That is a trophy shelf and this node builds one.**

### What answers it, and it is the only reason this build is defensible

**Every trophy on the shelf carries a route to its own round proof.** Design principle 1 applied to the one surface built against a node's advice: the shelf exists, and nothing on it is a claim rather than a record. A stranger can recompute any line.

**And there is no total.** No sum, no rank, no tier, no position against anyone. `D-25` rule 3 says a tier gives a case and nothing else. **A shelf with a total at the top is a leaderboard with one row.**

## 3. Four things the baseline's profile does that this node refuses

**One counter, not three.** The capture's banner reads Cases, Case battles and Gunfights, and two of the three read `N/A`. **Two modes that do not exist, rendered as empty figures, is the dead item defect with a number in it.** Round 1 has one mode, so the banner carries one count and names the absence in words. When a second mode arrives it brings its own count and its own list.

**No history tabs.** For the same reason and by the same rule as Home's four game mode cards, `D-19`: one live tab of four is the defect, so the carrier is not drawn until it has more than one live item in it.

**The name is not printed twice.** The baseline repeats it under the avatar. Two renderings of one fact is the defect class this project has now caught four times.

**The public id is not the account id.** The id on `5.10` is what support and an appeal refer to; publishing it hands a stranger the string an impersonation uses. The public id is a different value and **its format is `[?]`**. The node's recommendation is that it is opaque: the live product's is a six digit number a reader can decrement, which makes the registered account total readable from outside. Same class of leak `7.1` flags for the round id, and the same recommendation.

## 4. What the shelf lists, and it is not the inventory

**It lists what the account has won, not what it holds.** An item sent to Steam or sold back for coins leaves the account and stays on this page, because what happened happened. **A shelf that empties when a person cashes out is a shelf reporting current wealth**, which is a different and worse object than a record of outcomes.

**The item card is `0.6` with a stranger's field set.** No tick, no actions, no market foot: a stranger owns nothing here and may act on nothing here. What is added is the case it came from, the date, and the route to the round.

**The value shown is the value at the time it is read**, in coins, and what one coin is worth in real money is not published, `D-28`. This node does not print a value at the moment of the win: row `F2` was removed from the item card by `D-90` and this node does not reintroduce it on a public surface.

---

## 5. Indexation, and it is decided here rather than inherited

**`noindex, follow`.** `7.1` is noindex because indexing one object is pointless. **This page is noindex because it is about a person.** Letting a crawler hold a list of what someone owns is a publication decision nobody has made, and the owner cannot turn this page off yet, so the least the product does is not hand it to a search engine.

**There is no directory and no search.** The page is reached from a drop in the live strip or from a shared result. **The shelf exists and is not enumerable**, which is a real limit on the harm and is the reason section 3's recommendation about the id format matters: an opaque id keeps it that way, a sequential one does not.

## 6. State matrix

| State | File | What changes | Reader |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | `player.html` | Nothing. A stranger reading someone else's page | Stranger, no account |
| The owner reading their own | `player-owner.html` | Signed-in shell and the account band, a band at the top saying this is their page as a stranger reads it, a route back to `5.10`, and the `D-69` sentence sharpened because it is the person it concerns reading it | The account holder |
| Nothing won yet | `player-empty.html` | Counter reads `0`, the shelf is a rendered absence naming what would fill it | Either, and the page says which it is drawn for |
| No page to show | `player-gone.html` | One message for "no such account" and "not available", because telling them apart from outside is how an id gets enumerated | Stranger |

**The empty state carries no control for a stranger**, on the rule `history-cashout.html` set: a rendered absence makes no offer, and a dead item is an offer that is not kept.
