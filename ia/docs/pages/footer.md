## 1. The legal ground, opened live rather than recalled

Both quotes from EUR-Lex, opened 11 August 2026, `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32000L0031`, Directive 2000/31/EC on electronic commerce.

### Article 5(1), general information to be provided

> "Member States shall ensure that the service provider shall render **easily, directly and permanently accessible** to the recipients of the service and competent authorities, at least the following information:
> (a) the name of the service provider;
> (b) the geographic address at which the service provider is established;
> (c) the details of the service provider, including his electronic mail address, which allow him to be **contacted rapidly** and communicated with in a direct and effective manner;
> (d) where the service provider is registered in a trade or similar public register, the trade register in which the service provider is entered and his registration number;
> (e) **where the activity is subject to an authorisation scheme, the particulars of the relevant supervisory authority**;
> (g) where the service provider undertakes an activity that is subject to VAT, the identification number."

**"Easily, directly and permanently accessible" is the footer's legal job description.** Permanently accessible is what makes it a global element rather than an About page.

**The baseline already satisfies (a), (b) and (d) and the re-walk proves it**: "MIXABIT LTD, ΗΕ 470887, Elettherias, 19 Lakatamia, 2312, Nicosia, Cyprus". Registered name, Cyprus registrar number, geographic address, in small muted text under the tagline. **What it does not print anywhere is an email address**, which is what (c) asks for by name; the support button stands in its place. Whether a route to a form satisfies "contacted rapidly and communicated with in a direct and effective manner" is a question for counsel, not for this node, and it is carried rather than answered.

**None of those values crosses.** The operating entity for this product is a founder decision and every field is `[?]`. What crosses is the proof that the block fits and where it goes.

### Article 1(5)(d), the exclusion, and why it does not change the answer

> "This Directive shall not apply to: ... **gambling activities which involve wagering a stake with monetary value in games of chance**, including lotteries and betting transactions."

`CLAUDE.md` records that whether this category counts as gambling is unsettled and varies by jurisdiction. That question is open, has an owner, and is not this node's to answer.

- **If the product is not gambling**, Article 5 applies in full and the footer carries the identification block.
- **If the product is gambling**, this Directive does not apply, but then an authorisation regime does, and Article 5(e) already names the shape that regime demands.

**Both readings land on the same block in the same place.** The structure is decided; only its contents wait.

**Every band paints edge to edge and its content sits at the page measure.** That is the rule the whole shell now follows: the header, the main column and every footer band share one `padding-inline: max(gutter, (100% - 1220px) / 2)` declaration, so a band can carry its own surface across the full screen while its content still lines up with the columns above it. **Before `D-43` the cap sat on the app box with no auto margin**, so a wide monitor got the page pinned to the left with a dead strip on the right, and no band could reach either edge. `wireframes/docs/conventions.md` section 8.

## 5. SEO and accessibility

**This is the second interlinking plane, and it is the one that carries the long tail.** The rail carries three or four destinations. The footer carries every indexed node the rail cannot hold, plus the legal set. Together they mean **every indexed page is reachable from every other page in at most two hops.**

**No H1.** A global element is not a page. **`<footer>` with `<nav>` around each link column, each with an accessible name.** Four unnamed navigation landmarks in one region is worse than none.

**The accordions are collapsed for reading, never for existence.** Every link is in the DOM at every width and the accordion toggles visibility. **This is the same rule 0.1 applies to the mobile drawer, and it is stated twice on purpose:** it is the single most common way both patterns are shipped wrong.

- Each accordion header is a `<button>` with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`, not a styled `<div>`
- Every target at least **44 by 44 CSS pixels**, matching 0.1's AAA choice. The support button is a real `<a>` styled as a button, not a `<div>` with a click handler
- **The statistics are text, and the figure is not an image.** A number in a graphic cannot be read aloud, cannot be selected, and cannot be checked
- Each statistic pairs its figure with its caption programmatically, so a screen reader never reads "363010503" with no idea what it counts
- **Live figures do not announce themselves.** A counter that increments is not an `aria-live` region: an assistive technology user does not need the number read out every second. It updates silently and is read on demand
- The identification block is text, not an image. A registered address in a graphic satisfies nobody, including Article 5's "easily, directly and permanently accessible"
- Link text is descriptive on its own: "Refund and payments policy", never "read more" or "here"
- The compliance line is not `aria-hidden` and not decorative. It carries the age statement, which is a compliance statement and not ornament

**Indexation of what it points at.** `0.9` and `0.10` are indexed and carry schema. The private nodes are absent entirely.

## 6. Adaptive behaviour

**Mobile, base, 360px.** Statistics as a two by two grid, never a horizontal scroll and never a carousel: four numbers a person cannot see at once are four numbers that prove nothing. Then the brand block, full width, with the support button at full width under it. Then three accordions, collapsed. Then the interlinking accordion, collapsed. Then the bottom row stacked, compliance line first, then copyright, then the marks.

**Desktop, from 900px.** Statistics as one row of four. Brand column plus three link columns. Interlinking block as a flat list below them. Bottom row as one line.

**The compliance line never becomes an accordion at any width.** The one rule in this node that holds everywhere.
