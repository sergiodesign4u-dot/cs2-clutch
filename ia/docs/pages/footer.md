# Node 0.2. Footer

**Type:** global element. **Group:** `global`. **Scope:** MVP. **Cluster:** 0, global shell.

**Purpose.** Carry the product's legal identity, its compliance statements, its support route and its second interlinking plane on every screen, in one account state, at two widths.

**Jobs served.** No job of its own: like 0.1 it is a carrier. **Parent class:** the compliance constraint in `CLAUDE.md`, "age verification before any case interaction", "geo blocking informed by cited legal research" and "responsible play tooling", for the trust strip; barrier `B8-3` by way of row `G4` for the support and appeal route; a named legal requirement, quoted below and opened live, for the identification block. All three are legal parents under the three class rule.

**Sitemap transitions:** 1.2, 3.1, 6.1, 0.9, 0.10, and 0.4 for the cookie policy.

**This node grew the map.** The pack's rule is that a carrier may not promise a destination the map does not hold, and this one promised six. Two new nodes were registered before this specification was written: **`0.9` Legal and policy pages** and **`0.10` Support and contact**. The map is now 62 nodes, 49 MVP.

## 1. The legal ground, opened live rather than recalled

`CLAUDE.md` requires a real structure of the world to be grounded in data, and a legal requirement to be read from the text of the law. Both quotes below come from EUR-Lex opened on 11 August 2026, `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32000L0031`, Directive 2000/31/EC on electronic commerce.

### Article 5(1), general information to be provided

> "Member States shall ensure that the service provider shall render **easily, directly and permanently accessible** to the recipients of the service and competent authorities, at least the following information:
> (a) the name of the service provider;
> (b) the geographic address at which the service provider is established;
> (c) the details of the service provider, including his electronic mail address, which allow him to be **contacted rapidly** and communicated with in a direct and effective manner;
> (d) where the service provider is registered in a trade or similar public register, the trade register in which the service provider is entered and his registration number;
> (e) **where the activity is subject to an authorisation scheme, the particulars of the relevant supervisory authority**;
> (g) where the service provider undertakes an activity that is subject to VAT, the identification number."

**"Easily, directly and permanently accessible" is the footer's legal job description.** Permanently accessible is what makes it a global element rather than an About page.

### Article 1(5)(d), the exclusion, and why it does not change the answer

> "This Directive shall not apply to: ... the following activities of information society services: ... **gambling activities which involve wagering a stake with monetary value in games of chance**, including lotteries and betting transactions."

`CLAUDE.md` records that whether this category counts as gambling is unsettled and varies by jurisdiction. That question is open, has an owner and is not this node's to answer. **It does not have to be answered for this node to be built**, and that is the finding worth keeping:

- **If the product is not gambling**, Article 5 applies in full and the footer carries the identification block.
- **If the product is gambling**, this Directive does not apply, but then an authorisation regime does, and Article 5(e) already names the shape that regime demands, the particulars of the supervisory authority.

**Both readings land on the same block in the same place.** The structure is decided; only its contents wait. What waits is written down: the operating entity, the registered address, the register and number, the VAT number, and the supervisory authority if there is one, all `[?]` and all owned by the founder with counsel, riding on the same answer as `D-A`.

## 3. Anatomy, top to bottom

### 3.1 The trust strip, above the footer, on every page

One row, always visible, never collapsed into an accordion at any width.

| Element | Content | Parent |
|---|---|---|
| Age statement | 18+ only, stated as a fact rather than as a link | Compliance constraint, "age verification before any case interaction" |
| Responsible play | A link, not a sentence. Third entry after the rail and the money control | Compliance constraint, "responsible play tooling", plus `B7-4`, pattern of 12 |
| Market statement | That the service is unavailable in some markets, with the route to the list | Compliance constraint, "geo blocking informed by cited legal research". Row `B4` |

**One candidate was considered for this strip and declined.** The rolling median and p90 withdrawal time from our own logs, rows `A4` and `G3`, is the strongest trust signal the product owns and it is visible before login. Both rows say in their own cells: **"same feature as A4, two surfaces"**, and those two are the entry surface and the withdrawal surface. Putting it on every page makes a third. **That is a scope change, not a layout choice**, so it is named here with an owner rather than added quietly or dropped silently. **Owner: the founder, and it costs one line in the backlog if the answer is yes.**

**The strip is not a badge row.** No trust seals, no "100% secure", no invented certifications. Design principle 1 says trust is the product, and `D-14` names the limit on what a trust claim can do: what answers the doubt people actually record is published chance, current value and tested RTP. A seal answers none of it.

### 3.2 The main footer, four columns

| Column | Contents | Leads to |
|---|---|---|
| **Play** | Cases, Provably fair | 3.1, 1.2 |
| **Help** | Support and contact, the appeal route with its published response deadline | 0.10 |
| **Legal** | Terms of use, Privacy policy, Cookie policy, Refund and payments policy | 0.9, and the cookie policy is also where dialog 0.4 links |
| **Play responsibly** | Responsible play, the age statement in full, the blocked market list with its cited ground | 6.1, 2.2 |

**The footer links no private transactional node.** Deposit `4.1`, account `5.1`, withdrawal `5.3`, sign in `2.4` and identity `2.7` are all noindex and none of them appears here. A global footer is crawled on every page, and pointing it at noindex routes spends crawl budget on pages that decline to be indexed while putting private routes on every screen a stranger sees. **The rail already carries the ones a person needs.**

## 4. State matrix

A footer has fewer states than a header, and saying so is more useful than manufacturing some.

| Zone | Guest, desktop | Guest, mobile | Account, desktop | Account, mobile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trust strip** | One row, always visible | One row, wrapping to two lines, never an accordion | Same | Same |
| **Columns** | Four columns side by side, all expanded | Four accordions, **collapsed by default and present in the DOM** | Same | Same |
| **SEO block** | Flat list, expanded | One accordion, collapsed by default, present in the DOM | Same | Same |
| **Bottom row** | Identification, copyright, social | Stacked, identification first | Same | Same |

**No account dimension.** The footer is identical in both account states, and that is deliberate. Everything in it is public: legal identity, compliance statements, support, and indexed routes. A footer that changed on login would mean it held something private, which section 3.2 forbids.

### Transient states

| State | What the footer does | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Geo blocked**, `2.2` | The footer renders in full, and the market statement carries the cited ground for this visitor's market | `2.2` is a dead end by design. A dead end that also removes the legal identity and the support route is how a person with a legitimate complaint loses the ability to make it |
| **Gate open**, `2.1` | Inert behind the gate layer, not removed | Same rule as 0.1 |
| **Cookie consent pending**, `0.4` | The cookie policy link works before consent is given | A consent dialog that links to a policy the consent gate blocks is circular |
| **Boundary in force**, `6.3` | Unchanged. The responsible play link keeps its place and its label | A boundary is not an error state and the footer does not editorialise about it |
| **404 and 500**, `0.3` | The full footer renders on both | `0.3` says never a dead end. The footer is what makes that true on an error page: legal identity, support, and a route back |

## 5. SEO and accessibility

**This is the second interlinking plane, and it is the one that carries the long tail.** The rail carries three or four destinations. The footer carries every indexed node the rail cannot hold, plus the legal set. Together they mean every indexed page is reachable from every other page in at most two hops.

**No H1.** A global element is not a page.

**`<footer>` with `<nav>` around each link column, each with an accessible name.** Four unnamed navigation landmarks in one region is worse than none.

**The mobile accordions are collapsed for reading, never for existence.** Every link is in the DOM at every width and the accordion toggles visibility. This is the same rule 0.1 applies to the mobile drawer, and it is stated twice on purpose: it is the single most common way both patterns are shipped wrong.

- Each accordion header is a `<button>` with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`, not a styled `<div>`
- Every target at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, matching 0.1's AAA choice
- The identification block is text, not an image. A registered address in a graphic satisfies nobody, including Article 5's "easily, directly and permanently accessible"
- Link text is descriptive on its own: "Refund and payments policy", never "read more" or "here"
- The trust strip is not `aria-hidden` and not decorative. It carries the age statement, which is a compliance statement and not ornament

**Indexation of what it points at.** `0.9` and `0.10` are indexed and carry schema. The private nodes are absent from this footer entirely, so no `nofollow` is needed anywhere: **the correct way not to pass a link is not to draw it.**

## 6. Adaptive behaviour

**Mobile, base, 360px.** Trust strip first, as one wrapping row that never collapses. Then four accordions, collapsed. Then the SEO accordion, collapsed. Then the bottom row stacked, with the identification block above the copyright, because it is the part with a legal obligation attached.

**Desktop, from 900px.** Trust strip as one row. Four columns side by side, all expanded, no accordion behaviour at this width. SEO block as a flat list below them. Bottom row as one line with the identification block on the left and the social set on the right.

**The trust strip never becomes an accordion.** A compliance statement that a person has to open is not a statement. This is the one element in the node with a rule that holds at every width.
