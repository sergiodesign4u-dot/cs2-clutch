# Node 7.1. Public result

**Type:** page. **Group:** `pages`. **Scope:** MVP. **Cluster:** 7, tell someone.

**Purpose.** Carry one open, with its skin, its case and its round proof, into a stranger's browser, and require no account to check any of it.

**The newest surface in the product.** It is the ninth, approved by founder decision `D-20` on 11 August 2026, which took the round 1 scope line from eight surfaces to nine. `sitemap.md` raised the question at base-layer step 5 and refused to decide it, because `CLAUDE.md` locks the list and a rule outranks a step.

**Jobs served, and there are two.**

- **Emotional Job 2**, a win worth talking about, `jtbd.md` Section 3, Emotional and Social Jobs, closed by row `F4`, one-tap share of a result. This is the parent `D-20` acted on.
- **Related Job 3**, verify the outcome after I open, `jtbd.md` Section 2, Related Jobs. The tracing matrix found this one late and it is not a tick placed to rescue a screen: the job's own outcome clause reads "so that I trust the platform enough to open again **or to tell someone about my result**". That line had been in `jtbd.md` since stage 02 and nobody had traced it.

**Also the destination of every ticker tile in `0.8`**, which is the second job this node acquired. The baseline sends every tile to a stranger's profile at `/en/profile/<id>`, `baseline.md` section 3, and no job in this repository requires a profile object at all. **The social-proof surface now points at something checkable instead of at a trophy shelf.**

**Parent class:** job, twice, plus the founder decision that created the surface. Both jobs are legal parents under the three class rule.

**URL:** `/r/<id>`. **Indexed:** no, `noindex, follow`. **Canonical:** self. **Schema:** none. **Breadcrumb:** none. All five inherited from `0.13` sections 2, 3 and 4.1, and not re-decided here.

**Renders:** `0.14` variant V3, the full proof panel. `0.6` the canonical skin card with its instance value receipt. `0.7` the canonical case tile.

**Sitemap transitions:** `1.2`, `1.0`.

**State specified inside this node, under its own anchor:** `7.2` result gone or private.

## 0. What this page is, in one paragraph, because the object was undefined for four steps

Entity 15 in `sitemap.md`, Shared result, entered the map with **every part marked `[?]`**. Nothing in this repository said what leaves the platform when a person shares a win. `F4` is the single exception to the priority test in the whole backlog, the path does not break without it but the referral loop does, and **a capability that emits an object into the world with no definition of what that object is, is a gap.**

This node is the definition. **One open, one page: the skin instance that was won, the case it came from, and the round proof that settles it, all readable by somebody with no account, from a link they were sent.**

**And the primary action on this page is a verification, not a purchase.** The most-shared surface the product owns leads with "check this round yourself" rather than with "open this case". That is design principle 1 arriving at the one place where it costs revenue, and it is the reason this page is worth building rather than pointing the share at a landing page.

## 4. The share affordance `F4`, and what publication actually means here

### The thing this node found, and it is a real one

`F4` is described everywhere as one-tap share of a result, which reads as though sharing is what makes the result public. **But `0.8` says every ticker tile lands on `7.1`, and a ticker tile is a win the person did not share.** So for any open the ticker carries, the page exists before anybody taps anything, and `F4` hands the person a link to an object that is already public.

**Both readings were live in the map at once and neither was written down.** The node is specified as the superset, which is the treatment `2.7` got for the same reason: one branch is deleted later rather than the whole node redrawn.

| Question | What the map says today | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Does every settled open get a page? | `0.8`: a destination on every tile, and every tile lands on `7.1` | **Follows for ticker-eligible opens** |
| Which opens enter the ticker? | Nothing. `0.8` specifies the tile, not the selection | **`[?]`, owner node `0.8`** |
| So does `F4` publish, or only hand over a link? | Unresolved. For a ticker open it hands over a link. For an open the ticker never carried, it is the publication | **Specified as both, superset** |

**The consent consequence, stated plainly rather than smoothed.** If the ticker publishes an open the person did not share, then the page exists before any act of consent, and **the revoke control in block 7 is the only consent instrument on this node.** That raises revoke from a nicety to a requirement. `0.13` section 4.1 already reasons from the same place when it keeps this page out of the index: indexing turns public-if-you-have-the-link into findable-by-name, "which is a different promise from the one the share button makes".

### What a person can revoke, and what revoking cannot do

| Action | Effect | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Revoke** | The URL stops resolving as a result and renders `7.2`. Copies of the link already sitting in a chat resolve to `7.2` as well, which is the point | One response for every reason, section 6 |
| **The ticker tile** | Stops linking here. Whether the tile itself is removed is `[?]`, owner `0.8` | |
| **Publish again** | **Restores the same URL.** A second id for one round would put two proof pages for one object into the world, which the one-canonical-rendering rule in `0.14` forbids | Derived from `0.14`, not invented here |
| **What it cannot do** | Unsend a screenshot, or retract an unfurl preview already rendered in somebody's chat client. **The page says so rather than implying a power it does not have** | Own research |
| **Appearance** | How the account appears is the person's, per `0.8`. Changing it changes it here | `0.8` |

### What the card must never carry, and the reason is one rule applied three times

- **No value figure of any kind.** An unfurl image is frozen at render time and cannot carry an as-of, and `0.11` rule 2 requires one on anything that moves. **A price baked into a card rots silently while still reading as current**, and it is the exact failure `B7-1` describes: a number that describes a different object than the one received.
- **No multiplier, no "insane", no rarity boast.** `0.11` rule 7, a number is never a score, plus the survivorship refusal in section 2.
- **No verified mark.** `0.14` section 0, and an image cannot be checked, which makes it the worst possible carrier for a proof claim.

**The cost of this rule, stated rather than hidden.** The story a person wants to tell has a number in it, and the card refuses to carry the number. The card is weaker for it. **The page is one tap away and carries the receipt with its as-of, in text, where it can be read, copied and checked**, which is where a number belongs in this product. A card that travels further by carrying a figure nobody can check would be trading design principle 1 for a click.

## 6. Node `7.2`. Result gone or private

**Type:** state. **Scope:** MVP. **Anchor:** `7.2`. **Route out:** `1.2`.

**Three ways to arrive:** the owner revoked it, the account is closed or restricted, or the id never existed at all.

### One response for all three, and it is a privacy decision rather than a shortcut

Distinguishing "revoked" from "never existed" tells a stranger whether a particular person ever had a particular result, which is precisely what a revoke was meant to stop telling them. **So the three arrivals render one state with one message.**

**HTTP 404, not 410 and not 200.** `410 Gone` states that the resource existed, which leaks the same fact the message refuses to leak. `200` would be a soft 404, which `0.3` forbids outright. The page is `noindex` either way, so nothing here is an indexation trick.

### What it says, and the limit it has to admit

**It routes into `1.2`, where the round can still be checked without this page.** That is `0.14` state seven, inherited, and it is right: a round proof does not depend on this page existing.

**And here is the limit, named rather than glossed.** A stranger who arrives from a dead link is holding no server seed, no client seed and no nonce, so `1.2`'s verifier form is a form they cannot fill. **Routing them there without saying what it needs is a route to a wall with a friendlier colour.** So `7.2` states what `1.2` requires and who has it: the person who shared the link still holds the round.

**Whether the id in the URL is enough to look a round up on `1.2` is `[?]`.** `sitemap.md` gives `1.2` "the verifier form, the round input", and whether a round input accepts an id or only a seed set is not settled anywhere. **If it accepts an id, `7.2` becomes a real continuation rather than a redirect.** Owner: node `1.2`, at its own step. This node does not decide another node's input field.

### What `7.2` does not do

- **No search, no "browse other results", no recommended case.** Same refusal as section 2, and stronger here: a dead end is exactly where a product is tempted to sell.
- **No suggestion that the person contact the owner.**
- **The shell renders in full**, so the rail, the header and the footer carry the routes out. `0.3`'s rule holds here too: never a dead end.

## 8. Adaptive behaviour

**Mobile, base, 360px.** One column: the result with its image, the two value figures stacked with their stamps, the case row, the proof panel, the recompute action, who won it, the routes out. The owner's controls sit directly under the result rather than at the bottom, because the owner arrives to act rather than to read.

**The 64-character problem arrives here in its worst form**, `0.14` section 5, and this node inherits every line of it rather than solving it again: truncate in the middle and never at the end, the full value always retrievable by copy and by selection, monospace, always text, the accessible name is the field rather than the value, and copy is a first-class control because there is no hover on a phone and this string exists to be pasted somewhere else.

**No horizontal scroll at 360px, and the two things that would cause one:** the hash, handled above, and the ticket range printed beside the result on one line. The range wraps to its own row rather than pushing the panel wide.

**Desktop, from 900px.** The result and the proof panel can sit side by side, and **the proof does not shrink into a footnote when there is room for two columns.** Width is not permission to demote the evidence.

## 9. SEO and accessibility

**`noindex, follow`, inherited from `0.13` section 4.1 with its three reasons: scale, since one page per win is thousands of near-identical templated pages, which is thin content by construction; where the object actually travels, which is a chat and not a search result; and consent.** `follow` rather than `nofollow` is deliberate, because every result links to `1.2` and `1.0` and those are the pages that should collect whatever the link is worth.

- **No schema at all**, `0.13` section 3. And the two refusals in its section 7 land here directly: the skin is not an `Offer`, because we do not sell that skin at that price, and nothing on this page is a `Review` or an `aggregateRating`.
- **Exactly one H1**, and it is the result in words.
- **No breadcrumb.** `0.13` gives this node none. **`0.1` says the opposite in one sentence**, and that is a finding in section 10 rather than a decision taken here.
