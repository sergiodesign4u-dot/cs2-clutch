Case catalogue

The whole backed shelf, in a form where a person can narrow it to what they can afford and see what is really in stock before committing to anything. Deliberately off the main path: D-20 put the featured grid on Home, so this node is reached as all cases rather than as a compulsory hop, and its target is legibility rather than speed to the trigger.

What this node is not, and it is a decision rather than a preference

The browsing route is four taps. The main job route is three, and it does not pass through here.

Off the main path, by D-20

Home carries the featured case grid directly, which is what keeps the main job at three taps, and this node holds what Home cannot: the categories, the search, the filters and the daily free case as an addressable case. The map prices the difference out loud: the browsing route is four taps and over budget, and it is not the main-job route.

So the design target here is not speed to the trigger. The Opener who arrived from a clip is already on 3.3 by tap 1. The person on this page has a different question, which is what is on the shelf and what fits inside a budget, and that is answered by legibility.

The catalogue's size is [?] and no longer blocks anything. D-20: whatever the backed count turns out to be, Home shows a curated subset and this node holds all of it. D-D still sets that count, and it decides exactly one thing here, whether the category bar exists at all.

The daily free case is an addressable case, and the consequence is not free

An obligation written down before this node existed, and an orphan carried onto it rather than lost here.

What addressable means, concretely

It is a case. It has a tile, it sits in the grid, it carries the same four fields plus the daily marker 0.7 already holds, and it routes to 3.3 like any other case. The map's own words: I1 and I2 are components rather than screens, so the answer is not a surface, it is naming who owes it. Home owes the starter credit. This node owes the daily free case.

And the consequence, which is the part worth writing down: it can be filtered out of view

A banner survives every filter because it is not part of the list. A case does not. Filter to one category or to a price range and the daily free case leaves the grid with everything else that does not match. That is the price of the decision rather than a defect in it, and it is stated so that stage 04 does not quietly promote it back into a banner to keep it always visible.

Row I2 carries its orphan status onto this page

The daily free case is the only capability on the map with no parent in any of the three legal classes, placed in round 1 by founder decision of 11 August 2026. It runs against the residual risk named in the same document: a daily free open teaches, on a repeating schedule, the impression the rest of the map spends its budget contradicting, that opening is free. CLAUDE.md requires an orphan to be carried with its status printed in its own row. This is that row.

An inherited answer exists and is not adopted here. The baseline gates its daily reward behind a wager requirement, 0.00 / 5.00, and presents it as a five tier ladder. That is an answer to exactly this risk and it is the founder's to take or refuse. This node ships the case ungated and hands the question on, because adopting a wager requirement would be a product decision taken inside an IA node.

Zero stock stays visible, which is the whole of D1 on this surface

The one row in the bank that no competitor matches, and the reason is one sentence long.

An item at zero free units stays visible and marked rather than being filtered out, so the shelf is legibly empty instead of silently short. The flows state the reason in a line: hiding sold-out items would restore exactly the surprise D1 exists to remove.

On this page the figure is case level, and that distinction matters

The Case entity carries "free-unit availability across its table", A2, and the tile's stock marker is that figure, printed only when the shelf is short, D-24. A single item at zero free units is not a case level state, it is node 3.4 and it lives on the case screen, where the item card is. A case whose top item is out is still a case a person can open.

So there is no in-stock-only facet. At case level the predicate has no clean definition, and at item level the answer is already printed on the card.

The count obeys the register rather than a rule of its own. Every consumer of a published figure inherits all seven states of 0.11. The live free-unit count is a real-time row: a failing source renders as degraded and never as a frozen last good value, and missing renders as missing and never as a zero.

C. SEO text, finished copy

What a backed case means. Every item in every case here points at a unit we already hold. The roll can only land on an item that has at least one free unit, and the moment it lands, that unit is locked to the account in the same transaction that records the roll. This is why each tile shows how many units are free right now, and why that number can fall while you are reading it.

Why an item can show zero free units. When the last unit of an item is won, the item stays in the case and shows zero. It is not hidden. A shelf that quietly removes what it has run out of looks full at every moment and tells you nothing, and finding out afterwards is the thing this shelf is built to prevent.

The daily free case. One case a day opens without a balance. It sits in the grid with everything else rather than in a banner, so it is filtered and searched like any other case, and it shows the same chances and the same values as the ones that cost money.

This block stays the property of this node. Stage 05 aligns it to voice and syncs the final version back here. Interface strings do not move into it.

D. Structured data

CollectionPage plus ItemList. The members are the cases visibly in the grid, in the order they are visibly in. No Offer on this page: a list of links to cases is not a list of offers, and 0.13 places the offer on the case screen where the entry cost is the price of the thing bought.

E. Optimisation checklist

  1. Exactly one H1, and it is the page's job in words.
  2. H2s exist in block order, matching block B.
  3. Breadcrumb drawn, Home > Cases, with BreadcrumbList because it is visible.
  4. Every tile is a crawlable <a href>, and so is every pagination control.
  5. No figure is an image. Entry cost, item count and free-unit count are text.
  6. LCP is the first row of tiles. Below it, lazy.
  7. Canonical present, no hreflang anywhere.
  8. A category slug is real or the response is a real 404, node 0.3.
