Two loads on one screen

The constraint is inherited from the base layer, and this node is where it gets a shape rather than a defence.

The sentence this node was handed

"Featured cases sit on Home, so Home carries two loads at once: the trust evaluation, A1 to A5, and the entry to the main job. That is a crowded screen, and design principle 3 says cost never hides inside excitement. A home that is simultaneously a trust page and a shop is exactly where that risk lives. The compromise is accepted here and handed forward as a named constraint for the wireframe stage, not as a solved problem."

The answer is not a smaller page. It is a fixed order, so that the evaluation runs before the shop asks for anything, and the shop stays visible while the evaluation is still going on.

Why this order, and not another

It runs in the order a suspicious person asks the questions. Alive, then who, then what, then what it really costs, then is the roll honest, then can I get out, then what does it cost me to find out.

A third block was inserted on 19 August 2026 by D-27, and it is not part of the argument either. B4 sits directly under the hero because that is where the baseline puts it and because it answers "what can I do here" immediately after "who are you". It is the second thing on this page that is not evidence, and its cost is in the decision record: three of its four cards name something a person cannot do yet. They are drawn as not launched rather than as offers, which is the difference between marking a gap and advertising one.

Two blocks were inserted into that order on 18 August 2026 by D-25 and neither of them is part of the argument. The banner B2 sits between the ticker and the hero because that is where the baseline puts it, and it is the first thing on the page that is not evidence. The daily block B6 sits directly under the featured grid because it is a case offer and belongs with the case offers. Putting it any lower would have put a free entry after the starter credit, which is two free things in a row and the B4-2 failure twice.

The two trust blocks are ordered by the size of the pattern behind them, which is checkable rather than a matter of taste. B1-2, the buy-instead advice, is a pattern of 14 and the largest in the corpus, so A1 sits high at B7. B8-2, the withdrawal that goes silent, is a pattern of 6, so the exit block sits at B9.

The placement most likely to be argued with: the starter credit is last. Off the page since D-126 until its amount exists; the argument below is what it returns under

It is the activation lever, so the instinct is to put it in the hero. B4-2 is a pattern of 2 in which an implausibly generous starter gift is read as proof of a scam, and the To-Be map records the residual risk that the credit "teaches a first-session user that opening is free, which is the one impression the rest of this map spends its whole budget contradicting".

A free gift leading a page whose job is answering B1-1 is the documented failure mode, not the activation win. It ships, pre-login, on the page, after the product has been priced honestly.

B2. The promotion banner

  2. The countdown counts to a real, published end. A timer that resets on its own, restarts per visit or runs per visitor is a fabricated deadline, and a time is a number. If the end is not known, the countdown does not render and the promotion still can.

E. Optimisation checklist

  1. Exactly one H1, and it is the first thing after the shell.
  2. The H2 list matches the block order, with the two stated exceptions.
  3. No breadcrumb: a breadcrumb to yourself is decoration.
  4. Canonical self, no hreflang, host resolved once at production.
  5. Every route is a crawlable <a href>: every grid tile into /cases/<slug>, every ticker tile into /r/<id>, the shelf route into /cases, B8 into /provably-fair, B10 into /signin, B10 being off the page since D-126.
  6. Text, never an image, for every figure. They are the product's entire pre-login argument, and an image removes them from search and from a screen reader in one stroke.
  7. LCP is the hero or the first grid row, and never the ticker. A moving strip as the largest contentful paint makes the metric depend on an animation, and design principle 5 makes speed a product rule.
  8. CLS: every block reserves its height before its figures arrive.
  9. The meta description has a source on the page: every claim in it is rendered in B7 and B9 the Before you spend row and the grid, D-126.
  10. The accessible name of a figure carries its unit and its as-of, not the bare digits. Real-time counters update silently rather than announcing, and a degraded or missing figure is announced in text rather than by colour alone.
