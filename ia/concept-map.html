The project rule outranks the pack, and the CJM stage already proved the case: deposit closes no core job and stands entirely on documented barriers, `jtbd.md` Section 5, JTBD Matrix. Navigation model

Four global items in two states, levels assigned deliberately, and the taps counted.

Round 1 ships no left icon rail

The baseline runs a fixed 70px rail with nine destinations and exactly one of the nine is in our round 1. A rail carrying one live item and eight dead ones is not navigation, it is a promise the product cannot keep, advertised on every screen. Deferred with its trigger named, battles at Phase 1.5, rather than deleted. Removing it is also the flattening this step needed: the horizontal space it occupied returns to the reveal, which is the surface the product competes on.

Provably fair in the global navigation is a structural claim no competitor makes

`jtbd.md` Section 3, Emotional and Social Jobs names a public provably fair surface as one of three things no competitor offers pre-login, and the baseline walk verified on 11 August 2026 that our own baseline still redirects that route to login. Design principle 1 says trust is the product, and a principle that does not reach the navigation is a slogan. It carries the limit D-14 attached to it: its position is not a claim that a verifier converts a sceptic. What answers the recorded doubt is D2 and D4 on the case screen.

Pre-login the header is two items. My items and Balance describe nothing that exists before an account, so the pre-login navigation is Cases and Provably fair and nothing else. That is the shortest global navigation in the category and it matches what the pre-login job actually is: evaluate, then open.

Main job in 3 taps, and one of the three is the gate

Home, then a featured case tile, then the age gate, then Open. The compliance layer eats a third of the budget for the main job. It is also exactly why the gate carries only the declaration: D-17 puts identity verification before funding instead of here, and if that check sat at this tap the main job would be unreachable inside the budget for a first-time visitor.

The gate fires at first case interaction, not on arrival, and that placement is load bearing. B3 says "before any case interaction", not before any interaction. The tap count is identical either way. What differs is whether the product gets to answer B1-1, the category arrives pre-suspected, before it starts asking for things.

The browsing route is 4 taps and it is not the main job route. The Opener arrives from a clip wanting to open, not to browse 239 cases.

What was paid for three taps, stated rather than defended. Featured cases sit on Home, so Home carries the trust evaluation and the entry to the main job at once. Design principle 3 says cost never hides inside excitement, and a home that is both a trust page and a shop is where that risk lives. Handed forward to the wireframe stage as a named constraint. And one structural fork stays open: whether C1 survives as a separate node depends on D-D. A small enough backed catalogue means Home absorbs it and both routes become three taps.

Global
The four navigation items in their two states, the Balance figure once an account exists, and a footer carrying responsible play and the legal pages. Responsible play in the footer of every screen is deliberate rather than inherited: the baseline has zero responsible play routes and zero matches for self exclusion, deposit limit or cool down anywhere.
Moved at step 6
Responsible play is no longer deep. It was reachable through the footer, then the page, then inside it: three taps or more. B7-4 describes a pattern of 12 people inside an escalation loop, and a brake that has to be searched for at the worst moment is not a brake. It now carries a persistent entry inside the Balance control, two taps away and at the place the person already goes when they are about to spend. The constraint that makes this safe: promotion is reachability, not prominence. This route carries no counters, no streaks, no status and no session score, which is the same hard rule T4 attaches to the spend ceiling. The moment a limit acquires completion mechanics it stops being a boundary and becomes a reason to keep going.
Contextual
The age and geo gate at first case interaction, sign in at the first point needing an account, identity verification before funding, the post-reveal verification link and the one-tap share in the outcome phase, the spend ceiling at deposit.
Deep
The withdrawal restriction appeal inside S-E2, and the verifier form itself inside S-A2, since the page has to open and explain before it asks anyone to paste a seed.
Not global
The live drop feed renders on A1 and C2 and nowhere else. Pattern E requires it to be a layer on the screens where the core happens, and a feed on every screen would be wallpaper rather than context.

Five pairs worth reading

1. Twelve screen codes meant two things each

Before: C1 was the case catalogue and also "one real currency throughout". E1 was account and inventory and also "the reveal renders the settled roll". All twelve of twelve collided, and both readings parsed, which is why it survived four steps.

After: screens carry S-, backlog codes do not. Found by neither original pass: it surfaced while verifying a different claim against the file, which is the step the rules require before fixing anything.

2. The reveal could finish in the ledger and not on the screen

Before: the open went straight to the outcome. E1 settles the roll before the animation, so a dropped connection left a person watching an animation that never resolved beside a balance that said they won.

After: an interrupted state that names where the settled result waits. That is B6-1, the animation and the credited item disagreeing, which the whole of phase T6 exists to prevent, and it was arriving through the back door of a missing state rather than the front door of a bug.

3. A failed identity check contradicted a shipped capability

Before: drawn as an absolute dead end. After: an appeal that mirrors G4 and terminates, plus the withdrawal exit that B2 had guaranteed all along by forbidding any verification branch on the exit route. The diagram had been contradicting the map.

4. Two MVP screens had no route at all

Before: the catalogue and responsible play were both MVP screens that no flow passed through, so neither had a loading, an empty or an exit. After: flow 1a and flow 2a, both deliberately minimal. The catalogue draws only what survives decision D-D; responsible play draws the only flow in the file with no red node in it.

5. A screen disappeared between two steps and the count caught it

Before: the text read "seven orphan screens" and named six. After: Top wins restored. It was in the step 1 object list and had fallen out of the step 2 screen list, so the miscount was the symptom rather than the defect.

Withdrawn on verification, kept visible with their reasons

The Leave exit was filed as a dead-end defect and is not one. A visitor who evaluates the product and declines is the null result of a free evaluation, not a broken route. The finding was real about the documentation, not about the route: it was the only red node with no explanation under its diagram. Kept with the class corrected rather than deleted, because a silently discarded finding returns next round in the same words.

The colour legend inside the concept schema was filed as a page-content leak and is not one. A legend for reading a diagram is part of the diagram. The other card in the same section was analysis and did leak, and it was moved out.

Orphan job, first, because it costs more than an orphan screen

A job with no screen is a hole in the product: the person has nowhere to do their work. A screen with no job is ballast. RJ4, convert my result into something more valuable, has no screen in round 1.

Decision: backlog, deliberately, and the row stays empty and marked rather than being given a tick somewhere plausible. Its two screens exist in the map as LATER: Upgrade, whose parent is RJ4 directly, and Exchange, whose parent is a hypothesis. `jtbd.md` "Section 5" scores RJ4 at 2 for The Opener and 3 for The Accumulator, a secondary persona, and CLAUDE.md says the interface is not built around secondary scenarios even though they must work. This is that rule producing an empty row on purpose.

Orphan screen, and the thing the matrix cannot see

S-F1 Responsible play has no tick anywhere. Decision: keep the screen. The matrix is what is incomplete. F1 has no functional job and never will, because nobody arrives wanting to limit themselves. Its parents are barrier B7-4, a pattern of 12 people, and the compliance constraint in CLAUDE.md. Both are legal parent classes in this project.

This is a defect in the instrument and it is worth naming precisely. The matrix measures exactly one of the three legal parent classes this project uses, so any screen parented on a barrier or on a constraint shows up as an orphan. S-D1 Deposit escapes only by accident, because the CJM mapped phase T4 onto RJ2. Had it not, the matrix would have flagged the deposit screen of a money product as ballast.

The contradiction check the pack asks for, answered. F1 is marked MVP with no tick, which the pack says means either the mark is inflated or a job was not described. It is neither: the mark is correct and the job does not exist, because the parent is a constraint.

What the matrix found that step 2 had missed

S-G1 Public result closes Related Job 3, in that job's own words. Step 2 gave it a single emotional parent, which puts a screen outside this matrix entirely and would have made G1 a genuine orphan, weakening the case for it. Reading RJ3 in full closes that: "so that I trust the platform enough to open again or to tell someone about my result", `jtbd.md` "Section 2". Telling someone is in the functional job's own outcome clause.

This is not a tick placed to rescue a screen. It is a line that has been in jtbd.md since stage 02 and that nobody had traced. The scope question about the ninth surface stands unchanged, and it now stands on two jobs instead of one.
