Filter chip

A filter in force, said in words, and the cross that takes it off. It is one pill that holds a Button, the dialog's cross, and adds the words that cut with an ellipsis and the size of the cross. Everything on this page is drawn by design/system/components/filter-chip.css through index.css, the same file a screen will load.

When to use

A chip tells the person which filters are narrowing the shelf and gives a one-press way to take each off, so the shelf never narrows without saying why. It names a filter in force in the words the filter drawer used ("Under 10 coins", "High risk"), never a count or a score. When no filter is in force there is no chip and no empty row. The cross says what it removes in its accessible name, "Remove filter: Under 10 coins" (microcopy 3.1-35).

Rule and anti-rule

Use it

The catalogue's Filters in force: one chip for each active filter, each with its cross, and Clear all after the group.

Take another component

Choosing one of a few and keeping it chosen is a Segmented control. A small status mark with no cross is a Badge. A yes that stays until changed is a Checkbox. An act that does something is a Button.

States

No states: it is not interactive. The pill has no hover, press or focus of its own; the cross is a Button and keeps the Button's focus-visible ring and press (the Button's page), so no snapshot is repeated here.

Under 10 coins
  • One difference to know. The chip paints its cross in the body ink after the Button's own hover colour, so under the pointer the cross keeps its quiet ink and only its quarter turn shows. This is what kit.css draws today and the parity test carries it unchanged.

Read live from tokens.css. The pill reads the tile's veil and edge and the white ink; the cross's quiet face is the body ink. Geometry and type come straight from the primitives. No component token and no colour primitive is read directly.
