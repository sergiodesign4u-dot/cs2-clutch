How this case works

How a price buys one round and how a round is checked, beside a ruler of the 100 000 tickets: each item as wide as its chance in its tier's colour, a pin that runs to the round's ticket, and the item it falls in under the ruler's tear. Everything on this page is drawn by design/system/components/case-how.css through index.css, the same file a screen will load.

Anatomy

How this case works

The price buys one round, and the round lands on one item. Every item's chance is printed as a percentage and as a ticket range: the range is what the round resolves against, which is what makes a result checkable.

How a round is checked

The round is fixed before the animation starts and its hash is on screen when you open. After the reveal, one link checks the round against that hash.

How a round is fixed before you click

  1. We publish the hash of a server seed before the round is offered. The hash is a commitment: the seed behind it cannot be swapped later without the hash changing.
  2. Your round is settled once, from that server seed, a client seed and a nonce.
  3. The reveal plays back a result that already exists rather than deciding one while you watch.
  4. The server seed is revealed when the seed rotates, and from then on anyone can recompute the round.

Two reasons a round may have no proof to check yet: it predates the published ledger, or its seed has not rotated. Neither means anything went wrong.

When to use

How this case works stands where a player decides whether a result can be trusted: on the case screen under the numbers, and on Provably fair beside the check itself. It shows, with one real round, that a result is a ticket in a printed range, so the claim that a round is checkable is drawn rather than asserted (design principle 1). It never shows a verified mark: the words say what the proof proves.

Rule and anti-rule

Use it

Explaining what makes one round checkable, with the round's ticket on the line of 100 000: the case screen's How this case works, Provably fair's How a round is fixed.

Take another component

Every item with its chance and its ticket range is the Data table. A round's own values to recompute are the Proof fields; checking them is the Verify form. Text with no ruler is Prose. A question and its answer is a Disclosure.

States

No states: it is not interactive. The ruler plays once, set by the product's script (design/kit/motion.js, rulers): once it is half in view the script puts .is-on on .case-how__ruler; the pin runs to the round's ticket, the other items step back, the ticket's number counts up and the item rises into place. For less motion the script sets .is-on with .is-still, and the ruler stands at its end at once. No script loads on this page, so the three are drawn as the classes the script sets.
