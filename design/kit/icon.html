Icon

A glyph by name, drawn in the colour of the words around it and at their size, and its story form, a mark that plays a small story while its row is pointed at. Everything on this page is drawn by design/system/components/icon.css through index.css, the same file a screen will load. The whole sheet of glyphs, with the art, is on Icons and art; this page documents the component and does not repeat it.

Variants and sizes

Four axes, none of them a size class. Each carries its rule, so the next screen picks by the rule instead of finding a reason again.

  • Glyph = what it says, by name. 44 glyphs and the coin, one filled style (D-227). The set is on Icons and art; a new glyph is a new --icon-* primitive and one rule here.
  • Size = the words' size, never the icon's. The icon is one em, so the container's font-size sets it. The sizes the product uses are below, read from the render.
  • Colour = the role of the words around it. A glyph takes an ink role through color, so a theme moves it with its words. The coin is the exception: it is gold in both.
  • Form = plain or story. Plain is the mask. The story form is the same glyph redrawn in parts, and at rest it is the plain glyph.

When to use

A glyph names a thing at a glance beside its words: a destination in the rail, an act on a tile, a provider on its sign in button, a footer counter. It adds recognition and never replaces the label where the thing is an act or a destination: a glyph alone sits on a tile that carries its words for a screen reader, and a glyph beside a label is decoration and stays hidden from one (aria-hidden). The story form is the product's liveness: a row that answers the pointer with the thing it promises, a case opens, swords clash, a scope locks, a coin is tossed (design principle 2, D-235). It never carries meaning that the plain glyph and the label do not, because it plays only under a pointer or the keyboard, and not at all for anyone who asked for less motion.

Rule and anti-rule

Use it

The rail's row, a glyph on a tile at 22px, a provider's mark on its sign in button, a counter in the footer: one em of mask in the colour of the words beside it. A row of the rail wears the story form, because the row is where a person points.

Take another component

A money amount with its coin is a Figure, which holds the coin and the number. A status or a count in a pill is a Badge. A picture of a skin or a case is a Media slot. The product's own mark is the Logo. A glyph that does something is the Button's tile, which holds the glyph and owns the press. A payment method's coloured mark is a picture from design/kit/marks/, not a glyph.

States

The plain glyph is not interactive: it takes the states of the control that holds it, through the colour it inherits. The story form is the Icon's one state, and it is played by the host: its hover and its keyboard focus, .has-story:is(:hover, :focus-visible). It plays only where a pointer can hover and where the person has not asked for less motion; elsewhere every mark stays the plain glyph.
