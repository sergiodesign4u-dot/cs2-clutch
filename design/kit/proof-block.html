The round and how to check it, on the round's public page: its title and its state in words, the round's values in the dark hash well with Copy, the one line that says what the proof covers and what it does not, and the act. No verified mark anywhere: the state is a badge in words. Everything on this page is drawn by design/system/components/proof-block.css through index.css, the same file a screen will load. It stands on no coloured screen yet: the public result (node 7.1) waits for the rollout, so the block is built from kit.css and shown here alone.

Variants and sizes

One form. One axis, content: the round's state, which changes the badge's words and tone, what the well holds, the scope line and the act; the plate never changes. Sizes are read from the render; the plate's sides narrow from 22px to 14px under 600px of the window.

  • Settled (result.html): the state in the quiet badge, the six values, the scope line with its link to published against observed, and Recompute it yourself.
  • Matched (result-checked.html): Recomputed: matches in the green badge, the rest as settled. Shown with the first hash just copied: the answer line, Copied, is the product's script's (microcopy R155), green on the plate in both themes.
  • Mismatched (result-mismatched.html): our failure, said so in the red badge; both results printed (the settled 417 in the six values and the recomputed 30 211 under them); Report this round first, Recompute it yourself beside it.
  • No proof (result-noproof.html): the round predates the published ledger. Why in a rule line, no seeds, Read what the proof covers.
  • Failed (result.html?round=glockfn): the source cannot be read this minute. Why in a rule line, Try again.

When to use

The Proof block stands once, on a round's public page, the surface a result travels on: anyone who receives the link can recompute the round without an account (microcopy 7.1-2). Provable fairness is built so the product's own numbers are checkable (design principle 1, D-14), so the block hands over the values and says in one line what they prove and what they do not; it never says verified, certified or guaranteed. Our own failure is printed here, in the same block, with both results (wireframes/fair.html, 0.14).

Rule and anti-rule

Use it

One round's proof with its state, its values, its scope and its act, on the round's public page.

Take another component

What each field of a proof means, with no round's values, is the Proof fields in its default form (Provably fair). Typing values in to recompute a round is the Verify form. One hash under the open act is a Copyable value. A row of past rounds with their state is the Data table's ledger.

States

The block's one part a person reaches is the scope line's link, with a focus-visible ring of its own, white on the plate in both themes (--line-focus-on-plate), with its 4px corner as kit.css draws it. It has no hover: kit.css draws none, and the underline already says it is a link. Live at rest, on this page's theme: reach the link with Tab.

In the code

TokenKindDarkLight

Read live from tokens.css. Geometry and type come straight from the primitives; the one pixel literal no primitive equals, the plate's 22px sides, is a custom property of the block (--proof-pad) with its origin in the file. The plate keeps its colours in both themes (D-266, Q3), so everything the block draws reads roles that keep their value: the plate's blue, stripes, lit edge and drop, its white words, the link's ring. The scope line's white at .9 reads the role --text-lead-on-plate, shared with the Proof fields' key (D-267, Q2), where --text-lead would turn dark in the light theme. No colour primitive is read directly.

In the light theme the plate, the well, the title, the scope line and its link stay as in dark, and so do the well's lines and seeds in their on-plate forms (D-266). Two held atoms keep their own light look, readable but parting from the dark one; the block may not restyle them, so they go to step 7 with the Copy button on a plate D-266 already listed: the Button's ghost (Copy, the second act) turns to a white veil with dark words, and the Badge's status tone to a light tile with dark words. The green badge takes the light green of --bg-match in the light theme, the Badge's own look too; the primary act and the red badge keep their colour.
