Everything on this page is drawn by design/system/components/money-strip.css through index.css, the same file a screen will load.

It holds three atoms: Button, Icon and Note line, and restyles none. What is the molecule's own is the plate, the panels, the figures and the round cards. The figures are not the Figure atom: their size is read off the strip's width (--money-strip-fig), which the Figure does not draw (see the page's questions in the round's report).

Variants and sizes

Two axes. Heights and widths below are read from the rendered blocks. The strip is a container, so its layout answers to its own width, not the window's: the same block fills a column on Home and a narrower one in a dialog.

  • Content = what the block says. Before you spend (Home, the default) is the stub, three panels and the second ticket. What this case pays (--pays, the case screen) is the stub with a dollar mark, four panels (the tested return beside the case, the expected value, the chance to get the price back, the value against a real copy) and a note under it. Design principle 3: the numbers a player needs before spending are on the screen, in words and in figures, never hidden in the excitement.
  • Size = the container's width, by three cuts. Under 980px of its own width the pays block goes to two panels a row; under 820px the stub becomes a bar across the top; under 576px the figures fold; under 480px they stack in one column.
  • A missing figure (__v--missing, D-124): where a value could not be read the panel says "Not available" in white and smaller. It is not a zero and not a muted grey (D-254).

When to use

A money strip stands where a player is about to spend and is told what the spend is worth, on the screen where the decision is made: on Home before the first case, on the case screen before an open. It prints the tested return, the expected value at this price and the chance to get the price back, side by side, so none of them is read alone. It uses the numbers as published and says where a number is the platform's ("In coins at our values", microcopy 3.3-57). It never sums balance and value of items held into one figure and never calls a number a score (D-28).

Rule and anti-rule

Use it

"Before you spend" on Home and "What this case pays" on the case screen: figures with the line that says what each is, one link on from each panel that has somewhere to go.

Take another component

One number with its caption is a Figure. A pair of key and value in a card is a Key-value line. The facts of one drop (chance, tickets) are a Drop card. A table of every item with its chance and its value is a Data table. A single line under a block is a Note line.

States

Live at rest, on this page's theme: point at the panels and the round cards, reach them with Tab. The strip is not a control; the links in it are. A panel that is not a link (the case screen's) shows no hover wash, but the RTP panel still lifts its case.

In the code

TokenKindDarkLight

Read live from tokens.css. Geometry, type and motion come straight from the primitives; every pixel literal that no primitive equals is a custom property of the block (--money-strip-*), each with its origin in the file. The plate keeps its colours in both themes, so every ink on it is a role of the on-plate kind. Colours read in this block only, waiting for step 9: --gradient-spend-stub, --shadow-card-drop, --shadow-word-text, --color-plate-ticket, --color-ruler, --color-ruler-range, --shadow-ruler-mark, --filter-card-skin, and --color-plate-rule, one of the six with one reader per surface.
