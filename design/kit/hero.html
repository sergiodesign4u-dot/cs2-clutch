Home's H1 in capitals and, for a guest, the Trustpilot line as a badge. The H1 says what the place is and the badge gives the one figure that can be checked elsewhere (design principle 1, trust is the product). When to use

Use it as the first words of Home, under the event banner: an H1 that names the product by what it does, "CS2 case opening with published odds", and a third-party score that a person can check. The numbers are the site's to prove, so the badge is a sample until the real count is wired (D-124). The research's riskiest assumption is that a trust signal in the hero moves a first-time visitor towards a deposit (research/docs/lean-ux-canvas.md section 7); it is an assumption and not a result here.

Rule and anti-rule

Use it

Home's heading block: one H1 and, for a guest, one trust badge. One per page.

Take another component

A section's heading with its tile is a Section head. An event headline is the Event banner. A trail of pages is a Breadcrumb. A line that leads onward is a Text link. A claim with no figure behind it is not a badge: the badge carries a score, a source and a count, or it is not drawn.

States

No states: it is not interactive. The badge is a picture (role="img", no href), so it takes no focus and no press, and nothing here is snapshotted.

  • Under the pointer the badge lifts 2px, its edge brightens with a green glow (--hero-trust-depth-hover) and the five tiles hop in turn, 45ms apart. This is motion on a picture, carried as kit.css draws it (D-235), and not a control's state. Under reduced motion the badge does not lift, the tiles do not hop and the light does not cross.
  • A light crosses the badge every seven seconds. kit.css also writes a focus ring on the badge, --line-focus here; the badge cannot take focus today, so the rule is carried and does nothing until the badge becomes a link.
