Item card

One skin a person holds or held: its picture in its tier's hexagon, the gun, the skin and the wear, its value in gold, and what the place adds. On My items it carries a tick, Share this round and acts that open over the card; in the ledger and on a public shelf it carries a status band and Check this round. Everything on this page is drawn by design/system/components/item-card.css through index.css; names, wears and values in the samples are samples.

Variants and sizes

Three axes. Each carries its rule, so the next screen picks by the rule instead of finding a reason again.

  • Tier = data, set by the page. The card never decides its grade: the page writes --item-tier from the rarity ladder (--bg-rarity-1 rarest to -7), and the wash, the line on the edge, the hexagon and the wear's step follow. With nothing set the card takes step 5.
  • Content = where it stands. My items: the tick and Share on top, the acts over the card, the facts and what taking it out costs. The ledger and a public shelf: a status band, the value when won, where it came from and when, Check this round across the foot; the two are drawn alike.
  • State = what has happened to it. Chosen (ticked), sending to Steam, sold, gone. Each changes the band or the edge and nothing else. A sold card is its own receipt: no tick, no acts, its band says what happened.
  • Width = the grid's. The card has no width of its own: the Item grid sets the column, and its height follows what it holds. Read from the render below.

When to use

A skin a person owns or once won, shown as a thing to act on or to read about: My items, the history's Items tab and a player's shelf. The value is the card's one gold figure; what taking the item out to Steam costs shows with the acts ("Out to Steam", D-128), so nothing is spent behind a pretty card (design principle 3). The acts are the voice's plain verbs, "Sell for coins", "Send to Steam", and Exchange says it is not here yet instead of hiding (D-239).

Rule and anti-rule

Use it

A skin the person holds: a card in My items with its tick and its acts, or in the ledger with its band and Check this round. The page sets its tier and its state; the card draws the rest.

Take another component

An item of a case's drop table, with its chance and its tickets, is the Drop card. A win in the live strip is the Recent drop card. What came out of a round is the Outcome card. An item on its way to Steam, on the withdrawal screens, is the Withdraw item. A glyph alone is an Icon and a press is a Button; the card never draws either itself.

States

Live at rest, on this page's theme: point at the card, press it, reach it with Tab, tick it. The acts, Share and what taking it out costs show with the pointer, with focus anywhere inside the card, or with the tick.

Snapshots of the same instances, dark on the left and light on the right, taken with reduced motion so each shows the face the state settles on. The base set is shot once on this card: hover, focus on the native tick (the element Tab reaches), and the ticked card as a twin at rest, set by the product's own :checked.

  • The same answer, said once: the ledger and the shelf have no tick and no acts, so they have no state of their own beyond the pointer's lift; sending, sold and gone are classes the page sets and are shown in Variants.
  • The acts and the tick are atoms. A Button under the pointer, in focus or pressed does what its page says; the Checkbox does too. The card writes no hover, focus or press for either. Its own states are the lift, the lit edge and the skin that straightens, the ticked edge, and Share's tile and ring (Share shows only while the acts are open, so it is not shot alone).
  • The lift is carried as drawn. Under the pointer the card rises 3px, as kit.css draws it since D-241, where the rule for a state is ground, colour or edge and never position. Said here so the gate can keep it or take it out.
  • No disabled look of its own. Exchange is the one disabled Button of the product (D-239), the Button's.

In the code

TokenKindDarkLight

Read live from tokens.css. No component token: the card's face, its wash and its words read the semantic level, and the tier is the page's --item-tier. Two colour primitives are read directly and wait for step 9: --color-pink-on-dark (the pink of a card on its way to Steam) and --color-line (the band's rule on a sold or gone card, one of the six primitives with one reader per surface). Geometry, type and motion come straight from the primitives.
