Round 1, dropped because

  • A, the ledger. Hairlines and big figures on the ground read as a spreadsheet: no colour, nothing to press, and the block vanished between the daily cases and the prose under it.
  • B, the instruments. The gauges were drawings around a number rather than proof of it, and four equal cards in a row repeated the shape four times.
  • C, the ticket. The least bad, and its strip, stub, colour and big figures are round 2's A. Dropped as it stood: a flat paper coupon, four equal yellow boxes, nothing to click into and nothing on it that proved anything.
  • D, one hero figure. One number crowded the other three into a side column of text. Its idea of putting the case beside its RTP lives on in A and C; its idea of one large figure lives on in B, where the large one is a round anyone can check.
  • E, the departure board. Flip tiles are an airport, not CS2, and at 360 the tiles shrank until the figures stopped reading.
