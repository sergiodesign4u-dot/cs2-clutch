# Stage 06, step 1. References

**What this file is.** The techniques this product takes from outside itself, each with where it was seen, what exactly is taken and which recorded anxiety it answers. **It is not where the look comes from.** Since `D-181` the look is daddyskins.com's own, collected in `research/docs/baseline.md` section 13, and this file only names what raises its finish. `D-182` split the references by job: skin.club and skin.fans are the level to aim at, walked live; the reveal comes from the out-of-category benchmark of `research.md` section 5a, through Refero.

**Visible place.** The section "Brand and references" on `design/concept/concept.html`, built at step 5, renders the source table of this file. Until step 5 this file has no page, and that is the pack's order rather than a gap.

**Walked on 19 October 2026.** Public pages, nothing logged into, Playwright, computed styles of every visible element, the same collector as the seventh baseline walk. Screenshots in `design/concept/screens/`, prefixed `ref_`. A fact about a competitor below comes from those pages, opened that day.

## 1. The rule, from the instrument itself

The pack asks for Refero's own instruction quoted here, so that the rule of the stage stands on its first source. The style search, `refero_search_styles`, **returned no results on every query that day**, including the example queries of its own documentation, so no full style response was read. The rule is quoted instead from the tool's own description, which carries it in both its search and its retrieval tools:

> "Do not copy a single style directly. Search several directions, retrieve multiple strong styles, then synthesize a unique direction for the user's product." (`refero_search_styles`)

> "Do not copy a style wholesale. Treat each style as a reference ingredient." (`refero_get_style`)

Screen and flow search worked and are used in section 4.

## 2. What the base is

**The base is not a reference. It is the baseline**, `D-181`: Roboto, the inherited colour register, the inherited structure, brought to one value per job. Among the references, **skin.fans is the primary one for finish**, because the three things it does best are the three contradictions `baseline.md` section 13.5 names first: corners, buttons and flatness. skin.club gives two details. Refero gives the reveal.

| # | Technique, measured | What we take | Anxiety it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **One radius family.** Cards and buttons 14.4px, 153 game tiles on Home and 702 surfaces on the battles page; inner buttons 9px; header 12.6px; preview boxes 22.5px. Every value is a step of 0.9px from one base, so the product reads as built by one hand | The principle: one ladder, small, medium and large, every surface on a step of it. | S4 | **Rarity in two marks that agree.** A bottom-up gradient in the rarity colour to 70 percent behind the item, plus a thin horizontal line in the same colour under its name, `item-unit-line`, fading at both ends | The line. ## 5. One user signal that binds the finish

`cjm-to-be.md`, section "What carries into voice and into concept", records the corpus's only pixel-level remark about a reveal, `cjm-evidence.md` U-13c: **a gold rare-item display on a blue background "puts me off"**, said in the same breath as "ill scream like a monkey if i ever get one". One mention, marked as one. The baseline's top rarity step is gold, `#F6C915`, and its grounds are blue-violet, `#060A1E` to `#0F0E2B`. So the gold step is not to sit flat on a blue ground at the reveal, and step 2 carries this as an attribute rather than a taste.
