How this case works

The entry cost buys one roll. The roll lands on one item, and every item's share of the possible outcomes is printed on its row as a percentage and as a ticket range. The two say the same thing twice: the percentage is easy to read, and the ticket range is the interval the roll actually resolves against, which is what makes a result checkable afterwards.

How a chance and a ticket range fit together

A roll produces a number. That number falls inside exactly one ticket range, and the item holding that range is the item you get. The round is fixed before the animation starts, and the hash of it is on screen at the moment you open. After the reveal, one link checks that round against the number that was published before it ran.

What happens if a win cannot be sent to Steam

Every item in this table is available. The table is not backed by a shelf that can run short, so no item is ever unrollable and no row is ever greyed out. What can fail is the delivery, not the supply: if a win cannot be sent to your Steam account, it can be exchanged for an equivalent real skin or sold back for coins at the price shown here.

A row never disappears from this table, in any state. A table that quietly loses rows is a table you cannot check against what you read a minute ago.
